4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS
4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
Additional Financing and Restructuring: Since last ISM (October 2025), implementation progress has improved in key
areas. The recently approved AF of US$11.11 million — comprising a US$10.0 million grant from the Global Financing
Facility (GFF) and a US$1.11 million grant from the Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Multi-Donor Trust
Fund (HEPR MDTF) — brings the total revised project cost to US$131.11 million. The AF scales up critical activities in
Mpox surveillance, preparedness and response, as well as Reproductive, Maternal, Neonatal, Child, and Adolescent
Health and Nutrition (RMNCAH+N). A concurrent Level 2 restructuring was also introduced. The project closing date was
extended by six months to align with the regional MPA program; activities were reprogrammed to align with country
priority; and the results framework was updated to align with the MPA-level framework.The Project Development
Objective remains unchanged: to strengthen health system resilience and multisectoral preparedness and response to
health emergencies in Rwanda.
Implementation: The project is making reasonable progress following its restructuring and additional financing. Key
milestones include realigned priorities, updated procurement planning, preparation of technical specifications and terms
of reference, and strengthened environmental and social compliance. Results indicators have also been updated to align
with the broader MPA program.
Progress on Results: The project is making moderate progress toward its development objectives, though results vary
across indicators. JEE (2025) core capacity scores for Prevent, Detect, and Respond are all at 3, against a target of 3.5 by
2030. Laboratory accreditation (SLIPTA) stands at 25% versus an 80% target, as lab support only began in 2024. Some
indicators are on track — notably infant vaccination (Pentavalent 3) at 95% against an 85% target — but several others,
including specialized health security training and ISO lab accreditation, remain at very early stages. Overall disbursement
remains low due to procurement delays and pending assessments, and the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak declared in
neighboring countries in May 2026 poses an additional risk.

trajectory, while still maintaining an ambitious benchmark aligned with the project’s
objective.
