1.1. Project Description
The proposed project includes 3 components, including Component 1 and
Component 2 directly related to procurement of construction and
equipment, Component 3 relates to bidding for selection of consultants.
A brief summary of the three main components is:

98.95 million from WB’s fund).

in (i) construction of teaching and learning physical infrastructure

provision of cutting-edge appliances and equipment; (iii) upgrading of
foundational digital technology infrastructure (data centers, networks);
(iv) provision of support to faculty and students to adoptinnovative
teaching-learning methods; and (v) provision of support for program
accreditation activities and curriculum renovation activities.

2. OVERVIEW OF THE COUNTRY, BORROWER, AND MARKETPLACE
2.1. Operational Context
Vietnam has a remarkable development record - moving from one of the
poorest countries in the world to a lower middle-income country in less
than thirty years. Through sustained high economic growth and
supportive policies for the poor, Vietnam is moving towards poverty
reduction. Many non-income aspects of poverty, including health and
education outcomes, have improved significantly and the basic
macroeconomic fundamentals have been strengthened. On human
development, Vietnam has experienced substantial improvements in key

9

health and education outcomes: today’s population is far healthier and
better educated than 30 years ago.
a. Political aspect:
According to Worrld Bank's Vietnam overview report updated in October
2019, Vietnam's development over the past 30 years is very stable and
remarkable. From 2002 to 2019, more than 45 million people have
escaped from poverty, the poverty rate has dropped sharply from over
70% to below 6%, GDP per capita has increased by 2.5 times and GDP
growth rates, inflation rates. The transmitter is being controlled at a
stable level.
In line with international integration, the government has issued many
policies to enhance transparency and efficiency of investment and public
procurement environment such as: PPL No.43/2013/QH13 dated
November 26, 2013 provides for state management on bidding;
responsibilities of concerned parties and activities of bidding, the
Government's Decree No. 63/2014/NĐ-CP dated June 26, 2014 on
contractor selection, and the related Circulars guiding the
implementation of the Law and Decrees.This regulatory framework helps
ensure that the bidding law enforcement is standardized, synchronized
and unified, consistent with international best practices as well as the
commitment of Vietnam on bilateral agreements, regional and
multilateral, while efficiency and effectiveness of state management of
procurement activities have been improving.
PPL and regulations to significant extent meet the WB’s requirements for
national open competitive procurement:
(i) Mandate open competitive bidding as the default procurement method
and provide detailed procedures for open competitive bidding including
open advertising of the procurement opportunity and publication of
contract award information at the national level on the Government’s eprocurement system;
(ii) Provide sanctions against unacceptable practices including fraud and
corruption.
(iii) Procurement complaints are handled in accordance with the
procedures stated in Decree No.63/2014/ND-CP. These procedures
require complaints to be duly signed and stamped by a legitimate
representative of the complaining bidder. For complaints related to
contract award, the complainant has to deposit a fee whichwould be
cashed if the complaint is not substantiated. Complaints are handled by
procuring entities orthe investment owners and appeals are reviewed by
a competent agency.
(iv) The national regulations on procurement, construction and
investment also set detailed requirements on contract management with
available contractual standard forms and templates.

For 3 universities to join on project, procurement activities are governed

implementation.
b. Economy Aspects
Vietnam has achieved strong economic growth over the past 20 years,
moving from one of the low-income economies to the middle-income
countries. Since 1990. Vietnam has achieved the highest GDP growth rate
per capita (average 6.4%/year) in the early 2000s. Vietnam's economy
continued to grow strongly in 2015, at a rapid pace. GDP growth is
estimated at 6.7%. However, by the first half of 2016, due to the slower
global economic recovery than expected, global trade growth
plummeted, the economic growth in Vietnam was also affected by the
growth rate. GDP reaches 6.21%. To overcome these difficulties, in 2017,
the Government of Vietnam implemented policies to stabilize the
economy, control inflation and promote growth. By the end of 2018,
Vietnam's GDP growth has returned to a high level of 7.08%, the highest
growth rate since 2008. Economic growth gradually shifted in depth, the
proportion of total factor productivity (TFP) in GDP growth in 2018
reached 43.5%, the average period of 2016-2018 reached 43.3%, much
higher than the 33.6% average for the period 2011-2015. The ratio of
realized social investment capital/GDP in 2018 reached 33.5%.
According to statistical data from the General Statistics Office (GSO), the
inflation rate has remained stable for seven consecutive years, lower or
nearly 4% in recent years. Particularly, in 2019, with many uncertainties
of the market and the world, the inflation rate was controlled to increase
by 2.79% compared to 2018, this is the lowest annual average increase
in the past 3 years. The external balance is still under control and will
continue to be supported by an abundant FDI capital of nearly USD 18

11

billion in 2019, accounting for nearly 24% of the total investment capital
for the economy.
Exchange rate: according to the announcement of the State Bank of Viet
Nam (SBV), average inter – bank exchange rate between Viet Nam Dong
(VND) and US dollar as of 6th October 2017 was USD 22,470 and
increased to VND 23,500 in November 2019. The exchange rate will be
used as the basis for calculating total investment. In fact, the exchange
rate has increased steadily over the years. Therefore, the Vietnamese
currency is weakening against the USD. Therefore it will not adversely
affect the investment in the project.
c. Sustainability Aspects
- Compliance with the policies of the Government of Vietnam and the
Bank: The investment contents in the VNUDP project are in compliance
with the regulations of Decree No. 132/2018/ND-CP and in compliance
with cooperation orientationsin the Country Partnership Strategy (CPS)
between World Bank and Vietnam for the period of 2016-2021.
- Impacts on medium-term public investment plans: Under VNUDP is
proposed to be implemented for 6 years from 2019 to 2025, in which the
investment preparation stage is from 2019-2020 and the construction is
from 2021-2026. Thus, the impact on the medium-term public investment
plan for the period of 2019-2020 will not be affected because there are
no disbursement activities for the project in these years. The
disbursement will start from 2021 to 2026 and the impact of the project
on the public investment plan at this stage is considered to be negligible
as ODA funded projects have started to be disbursed and finalized. The
project will be implemented in 2019-2020 period and may not affect the
public investment plan for the 2021-2026 period.
- Social impacts: The project has positive impacts on the direct
beneficiaries of the project who are students, lecturers, and university
staffsin term of environment, health and safety because the
infrastructure such as learning centers, research institutes and
laboratories of the universities along with green spaces will be improved
substantially; Advanced fire and explosion prevention system will
respond instantly when incidents occur; Improving the wastewater
collection system, building wastewater treatment plants in the
universities to contribute to enhancing the quality of the effluent into the
receiving water and reducing water pollution. Besides, the adoption of
sustainable design will contribute to the efficient and economical use of
water and energy,reducing greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and
adapting to climate change. Gender inequality in universities will be
improved by upgradingproper facilities and developing incentive policies
for female students and female staffin academic activities and scientific
research in the universities.
It is further noted that currently, Vietnam is significantly impacted by
COVID-19 outbreak. While it is hopeful that this epidemic would end soon,
12

it would entail additional environmental and health protection measures
to be taken during project implementation.
- Impacts on people: The project of VNU-HN và VNU-HCMC will be built on
the lands that have been cleared and do not affect local households,
which is an advantageto ensure the project implementation on schedule.
70ha of land will be required affecting about 468 households and about
1,500 will be relocated. A Resettlement Action Plan (RAP) has been
prepared to address land acquisition impacts of the sub-project. There is
an estimated budget at approximately VND 1,221.7 billion (about USD
51.989 million) for land acquisition and compensation, and livelihood
compensation program; Besides, the clearance of unexploded ordnances
(UXO) in the land of 70ha for construction of UD sub-project has not been
completed. Therefore, UXO detection and clearance will be carried out
before the commencement of any construction work with more details
provided in the ESMP. This is also the risks during implementation of UD
project, and WB's safeguard policies should be strictly followed to ensure
entitlements of the affected households and people.
However, during the construction of the various infrastructure subprojects, contractors may mobilize a number of workers from outside the
project areas. This may generate potential social risks for students and
communities living in and surround the project area, such as violence with
local youth, gambling, drug proliferation, sexual exploitation, abuse and
harrassment and the risk of disease transmission (e.g., sexuallytransmitted diseases such as HIV, syphilis, etc.), particularly among
female students and local women. The mitigation measures for potential
labor influx will be contractually enforced through the inclusion of codes
of conduct in the bidding documents and the inclusion of necessary
requirements in the Contractor’s Environment and Social Management
Plan (C-ESMP).
- Environmental impacts:
+ Negative impacts: during construction, the project may generate noise,
dust, etc ... affecting the surrounding environment. However, these
impacts are insignificant and can be controlled by the environmental
monitoring and inspection plans by competent authorities.
+ Positive impacts: Through the project, essential infrastructure including
high-rise classroom buildings, internal roads, landscapes will be built,
wastewater treatment plant will be upgraded, and newly constructed in
three universities area. As a result environmental sanitation will be
improved, pollution of water, air and epidemics of students and lecturers
will be controlled thanks to construction of the wastewater collection and
treatment system.
The project meets the country's sustainable development goals as well
as the development strategy of three universities. During the
implementation, there will be some social and environmental impacts
from construction. However, with mitigation measures during project
implementation, these impacts are assessed to be minor and short term.

learning methods such as E-learning, massive open online courses
(MOOC)… have initially been applied by some education institutions in
Vietnam. However, these applications are still in small-scale and mainly
available at private schools or international schools, high quality schools
...while hardly found in public school systems due to the government's
regulations on public investment. Therefore, in order to access modern
higher education in the coming time, it is required to invest in both
information platform and conditions to access new educational
technologies in Vietnam’s education system.
For scientific research in education, particularly in the higher education,
information technology has been widely applied, high-performance
computing, calculation softwares, stimulation softwares ... have replaced
other types of traditional experiments. Nowadays, scientific research
works are greatly assisted by computer systems and information and data
systems shared on the Internet. Each year, Vietnam spends about 2% of
State budget for scientific research and this amount is allocated from
central to local levels, so the actual budget for scientific research at
universities is still very limited. Most universities are balancing their own
non-business revenues or funds from domestic and foreign donors to
invest in scientific research. Therefore, most laboratories are invested
with basic research and experimental equipment, while modern and
specialized research equipment are only provided for prioritized sectors
but with limited investment. Thus, it can be seen that there is already
market of machinery and equipment for scientific research in Vietnam but
mostly for basic machinery and equipment while specialized or modern
research equipment will need to be supplied from abroad.

c. Complaints Management and Dispute Resolution Systems
18

A number of issues related to the healthy conditions of the market such
as grievance redress mechanism are reflected in Vietnam’s legal system
for procurement. Complaints management and dispute resolution
systems are stipulated by relevant civil law. Procedures for procurement
complaint settlement is stipulated in Article 92 of the PPL. The PPL
provides a right for review for bidders. Article 91 of the Law stipulates
that every bidder who is participating in the bidding process has the right
to file a petition regarding the bidder selection results to the procuring
entity, the employer or the competent person about the issues relating to
the bidding process. It is clear that the parties handling the procurement
process, that is the Procuring Entity, the Investment Owner, and the
Competent Person are the same parties given the responsibility for
settling procurement complaints affecting them. This does not appear to
provide a fair avenue for the complainant. This lack of independent review
mechanism is a serious shortcoming in the transparency of public
procurement review procedures.
For this project, Three Universities will be responsible for settling
complaints in accordance with the relevant government regulations.
Though there is a formal system to handle complaint, in practice however,
Bidders hardly have confidence in the system and do not use the system.
According to the provinces participating in the project, no complaints
have been received or recorded under all their past projects. This may
due to the rigidity and cumbersomeness of the national complaint
mechanism and the fact that the law does not accept complaints to be
made anonymously as allowed under Bank funded projects. Given the
comprehensive requirements for the handling of procurement related
complaints under WB’s new procurement framework which governs this
project, the PMU will have to develop practical steps as part of the Project
Operation Manual (POM) to meet this requirement.

establish the PMU with two experienced procurement staffs and this

applied the Bank’s procurement procedures. The personnel involved in

Due to lack of experience in using WB harmonized procurement
documents for the NCB procurement method approved by WB, PMUs

often find it difficult to reasonably use these documents during the

8.1. Compliance with Contract Provisions:
Both the PMU and Contractors shall be responsible to comply with the
provisions in the contract signed by two parties. Any modifications of the
contract must be agreed in advance by two parties in writing and passed
by competent authorities and/or the Bank.
8.2. Supervision by PMU:
The PMU shall be responsible for supervising the performance of the
contractors/suppliers/consultants to ensure that they carry out the
contracts in accordance with the terms and conditions of the signed
contracts. The PMU must monitor the work progress, the timely
completion of work, delivery or supply of goods, and payments paid, and
determine whether the changes in the scope of works may be
appropriate.
PMU
should
request
the
construction
contractors/suppliers/consultants to regularly submit progress reports of
contract implementation for monitoring. PMU should designate a staff
with adequate technical qualifications, managerial experience to
regularly supervise performance of the contractors. If lacking appropriate
human resources, PMU can hire consultants to supervise the contract
performance of the contractors.
For goods and works contracts, the handover of goods and works must
comply with terms and conditions in the signed contract. The PMU must
provide valid documents and vouchers (certificates of origin, certificates
of quality, customs clearance, invoices, etc.). The acceptance and
handover of works shall be carried out in accordance with the contents of
the signed contract along with the minutes of completed work volume,
invoices, and valid documentary evidence recommended as the basis for
payment. Acceptance minutes along with invoices, and documentary
evidence will be the basis for conducting the predetermined payments to
suppliers.
For consulting services contracts, after consultants finish their duty, the
PMU must prepare a report on evaluating the results of tasks performed
by the consultant. The report and the contract liquidation minutes shall
be the basis for the contract completion.
8.3. Modifications:
PMU or contractors/suppliers/consultants may propose modifications to
the contract when either unforeseen events or the PMU’s decisions make
it necessary to modify the scope of work. If such modifications cause
changes in performance time or cost increase, the PMU must ask for
approval from competent authorities, including the World Bank (in case
of a contract with prior review) of these modifications. The PMU shall sign
the contract amendments or other documents appropriate with the
contractors/suppliers/ consultants prior to implementing such
amendments. All the amendments are prepared in the form of contract

35

adjustment appendix signed between the PMU and the contractors before
implementing the adjustments.
The adjustment of the contract (including the extension of the contract
duration) shall be carried out during the validity period of the contract. If
the contract has already expired, even if the adjusted contents have been
made in writing and signed by the representatives of both contract
parties, they will not be valid. The Bank shall not fund the payment related
to goods, works and consulting services completed after the expiry date
of the contract. Similarly, in case of the Bank’s prior review contracts, the
adjusted content shall neither be reviewed for approval nor receive no
objection from the Bank.
8.4. Unsatisfactory Performance:
Poor performance affects the quality, efficiency and economy of the
contract and it therefore shall not be tolerated. Based on the provisions
of the contract, the PMU should require the contractor/supplier/consultant
to carry out remedial measures immediately to solve the situation. If the
contractor/supplier/consultant fail to take adequate corrective actions,the
PMU may terminate the contract and apply penalties specified in the
contract to penalize the contractor/supplier/consultant.
