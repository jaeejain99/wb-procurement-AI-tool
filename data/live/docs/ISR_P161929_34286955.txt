 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Following are the key findings of the Nepal: Second Bridges Improvement and Maintenance Program (BIMP-II) performance, based on findings of

  the tenth implementation support review conducted from February 19-March 1, 2024, and completion of the Program's third restructuring in March

  13, 2024.

  Program for Results (PforR) performance: Good physical progress is noted under DLIs- 1, 2, 3 and 5. The DLI-1 (major maintenance works) has

  exceeded 5,000 meter (m) targets achievement and additional 162.03m targets have been completed. The Dossiers for remaining 537.97m bridge

  length is prepared and is in process of submission for verification. The revised target 5,700m is expected to achieve by April 2024. For DLI-2

  (bridge safety works) 5,225m has been achieved and the 2,775m remaining target of the disbursements is expected to be achieved in three

  tranches (400m by April, 1,000m by May, and 1,400m by June 2024) meeting the target of 8,000m. However, the review team advised to complete

  Dossiers submission to NPC by March to mid-April 2024, to complete verification before monsoon in May 2024. The budget deficit of approximately

  NPR 255 million to the maintenance contracts, is a risk due to lack of adequate cash-flow in achieving remaining targets of DLIs-1 and 2, by the

  program completion period. Under the DLI-3 (new 2 lane bridge construction) 3,415m is achieved against 4,400m and remaining 621m bridge

  length is under DLI verification and expected disbursement by March 2024. Similarly, the Bridge Branch (BB) is in process of submitting the last

  tranche of Dossier (364m bridge) to NPC by March 2024. In DLI-5 (backlog bridges), 7,300m target have been achieved and additional bridge

  length of 53.65m have been completed. The Dossiers for 1,211.35m bridge length is prepared and in process of submission for NPC verification.

  With that, it is expected to achieve complete disbursements, by April 2024. The DLI-6 (Standard Design Build: SDB) disbursement has been

  completed however, the SDB bidding documents is pending approval from the Public Procurement Monitoring Office (PPMO). Similarly, there has

  been noticeable progress under DLI-7 (Bridge Site Monitoring System: BSMS) with 2,759 entries results achieved. A total of 4,071 entries has been

  recorded till date in the system. Considering the over achievements of the targets in DLIs-1,3 and 5, the overall progress, the rating for the PforR

  DLIs is upgraded as Satisfactory.

  Investment Project Financing (IPF) performance: The progress under the Investment Project Financing (IPF) component is slow with only

  US$0.52 million disbursements till date. About US$7.61 million funds are now reallocated to the PforR DLIs. some of the targets under IPF-1, will be

  dropped due to time period limitation. The review team noted the DoR’s commitment to resolving NVC’s Non-Conformance Reports reports under

  (IPF-2). Under (IPF-3), two locations for Advanced Bridges have been identified at Narayani and Bijaypur. Bridge design and Environmental and

  Social Screening of these two bridges are being undertaking by the Design and Advance Technology Cell (DATC) and are expected to conclude

  within the program closing date. It is noted that DATC is comprised of 17 core members, out of which 41% are women. The International Bridge

  Consultant Under (IPF-5) is onboard and is supporting DATC in the design of the two preceding bridges. The procurement of various supervision

  and oversight consultancies including Bridge Structural Engineering, Geo technical Engineer, Hydrologist and Bridge Designer is in process. The

  operational and logistics support to DATC is ongoing (IPF-6). Under IPF-9, the baseline survey is completed, and draft report has been reviewed

  and provided comments by the Bank team, which is expected to be revised by March 30, 2024. With the cancelation of the delayed activities, the

  likelihood of achieving the the current planned IPF activities has been assured and accordingly, the overall progress of IPF component is upgraded

  to Moderately Satisfactory.

  Restructuring. The third restructuring of the Program completed in March 13, 2024, made the following key changes: (i) IDA US$7.56m IPF funds

  have been reallocated to PforR DLIs (1 and 5), without any cancellation of IDA support, and (ii) calibration in the DLIs targets have been made (DLI-

  1 increased from 5,000 to 5,700m, DLI-3 increased from 4,000m to 4,400 m and DLI-5 increased from 7,300 m to 8,565 m).

  RiskComments

  Comments

  he following are explanation provided for the change in risks categories and the High and Substantial rated risks.

  Political and Governance: Substantial. Nepal’s political situation remains uncertain with a fragile coalition government impacting both the federal

  and provincial levels of government. The federal system provides opportunities to decentralize development benefits and make service delivery

  more effective and accountable. Nevertheless, the risks of political uncertainty, jurisdictional overlap between the three tiers of government, and

  need for more clarity and coherence between policies and devolved powers will remain in the coming period. While the extent to which political risks

  can be mitigated through project-specific measures is limited, the World Bank will maintain continuity in policy dialogue with the government, and

  project design will include specific actions, as required, to build public support through broader stakeholder participation, strategic communication,

  and outreach. Accordingly, the overall political and governance risk and its potential impact on the Project activities is rated as substantial.

  Macroeconomic: Substantial. The forecast is subject to multiple downside risks. An erratic monsoon and natural disasters could dampen

  agricultural growth. Political uncertainty could hold back private investment and delay public capital expenditures. A renewed spike in commodity

  prices or continued food export bans by India would raise prices and widen the current account deficit. Higher inflation could keep policy rates

  elevated, increase domestic debt servicing costs, and drag on growth. Delays in pivoting fiscal revenues away from import taxation and towards

  domestic revenue sources would leave fiscal revenues vulnerable to swings in trade flows. Accordingly, the macroeconomics risk impact on the

  Project is retained as substantial.

  Fiduciary: Substantial. The residual fiduciary risk under the Program continues to remain substantial. The risk stems from the potential fraud

  and corruption issues likely to face the Program, which could potentially impede achievement of development objectives. For bridge works, key

  concerns relate to collusion or manipulation of bidding processes, fraudulent claims for payment against quantity items, deficient quality combined

  with inappropriate payments for completed works, and erroneous contract variations. To mitigate these risks, the Program has placed additional

  strengthening measures through the Program Action Plan. These include: (a) verification of Bridge Dossiers and physical inspection of Program

  bridges to trigger disbursements, (b) technical auditing by the NVC and subsequent referral of serious non‐ compliances to Nepal’s

  Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority, (c) use of the BSMS which offers enhanced means of quality control on remote worksites.

  Environment and Social: Substantial. The residual risk continues to remain Substantial. There are various potential E&S risks associated with the

  Program such as, vulnerability of works to natural disasters and climate change, potential exposure to child labor, and labor influx including GBV and

  risk of SEA. To mitigate these risks, the Program Actions Plans has been devised with the multi‐tiered approach, that is being followed by the

  implementing agency, as a part of the legal covenants, while implementing the Program. The key implementation support plan also includes a pillar

  on labor risk management focus on monitoring enforcement of contract provisions relating to labor camps, workers’ conduct, prohibition of

  child labor, and access to resources for communities that may be affected.

  Overall: Considering the ratings of various risks outlined above, the overall residual risks remains Substantial.
