3. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 3.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
   1. The NEHEP Program strategically supports a subset of the Government's National Higher Education Program (NHEP) of
   US$1.15 billion for six years including the base year (2021). NEHEP is financed through a US$60 million IDA Credit,
   comprising two components: Component 1 (US$57 million) uses a Program for Results (PforR) financing modality, and
   Component 2 (US$3 million) is financed through Investment Project Financing (IPF). The PforR component supports
   through Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) under four Results Areas clustered around six DLIs and 18 disbursement
   linked results (DLRs).
    2. PDO Performance. The Program Development Objectives (PDOs) are 'to strengthen labor market relevance and quality
   of higher education, boost collaborative research and innovation, and enhance equitable access for underprivileged and
   disaster affected groups.' Overall, the Program is making good progress toward achieving these objectives. Good progress
   has been observed under Results Areas 1, 2, 3, and 4, particularly in research, entrepreneurship, performance-based
   grants, support for underprivileged students through poverty-targeted scholarship, and increasing the share of students
   in online teaching and learning. These achievements contribute to PDO Outcome 2 (to boost collaborative research and

  innovation) and Outcome 3 (to enhance equitable access for underprivileged and disaster affected groups) with indicators
  5 and 6. Progress on PDO Outcome 1 (To strengthen labor market relevance and quality of higher education) has also
  gained momentum over the last 4-5 months, contributing to indicators 1, 2 and 3. Key developments include:
  introduction of LMDPs is being accelerated to contribute to indicator 1 and meet EOP target; the associated indicator 2
  (number of students completed first year in LMDP) is in good shape; indicator 3 (number of HEIs/programs accredited/
  completed peer review) is also making modest progress and expected further improvement with UGC readiness to launch
  program accreditation - program accreditation framework in place and its implementation manual is at the final stage for
  approval and implementation. Despite these positive developments, PDO performance rating has been downgraded to
  Moderately Satisfactory considering the potential negative impact of the ongoing implementation delays due to the
  absence of UGC Member Secretary (MS). Regular follow-ups by the UGC under the MS’s leadership, with university
  leaders, Deans, colleges/ programs are critical to expediting overall implementation – particularly additional focus on
  indicator 1 and indicator 3.
  3. Implementation Progress Performance rating remains as Moderately Satisfactory. The prolonged absence—
  approximately five months—of the UGC MS, who also serves as the NEHEP Program Director and is responsible for
  overall program operation, has slowed policy dialogue, and implementation of key reforms, and the completion of agreed
  actions from the 7th review mission aimed at accelerating progress. The UGC has authorized a Director to act on behalf of
  the MS in matters related to financial operations, including the signing of withdrawal applications. This interim
  arrangement will enable progress on several pending issues. However, it does not substitute for the MS’s critical role in
  advancing key reforms and leading policy dialogue with high-level officials of universities, Government and other
  stakeholders. A new MS is expected to be appointed by June 30. Additionally, delays in appointing a new Vice Chancellor
  of TU—which accounts for 75 percent of total higher education enrollment—may further hinder higher education key
  reforms. Interest groups and opponents of the reform agenda have raised concerns about the legitimacy of key reforms
  being commenced under the current VC appointed for temporary 3-month term to oversee regular operations. These
  groups may pose additional obstacles to implementing ongoing reforms. While these appointments are beyond the
  control of the implementing agency, the delays are negatively impacting on the overall progress of higher education
  reforms and NEHEP implementation. The team will reassess the rating in August, and it may be downgraded if the
  appointments are still pending and implementation remains stalled.
  4. Progress on other indicators are intact or improved: safeguards- improved, Financial Management-intact,
  Procurement-intact, and M&E: intact. Two safeguards consultant hired during this period at UGC have made visible
  contributions in strengthening UGC and HEIs capacities on safeguards. Procurement section with support of the UGC
  procurement officer and a consultant have been providing procurement training and support to HEIs including
  implementation of eGP. Progress on Financial Management is also on track with no overdue reports. Implementation of
  Intermediate Results Indicators (IRI) and Program Action Plan (PAP) are also largely on track.
  5. Citizen Engagement, Gender and Climate Change. UGC has been regularly organizing consultations with stakeholders,
  especially with universities, campus management team, students, teachers, alumnae and other stakeholders. Different
  stakeholders participated in the citizen engagement since the 7th review meeting in November 2024 include:
  digitalization, annual planning and management - 1,200 participants from 200 HEIs; safeguards, Code of Conduct and
  grievance handling – 300 participants; performance based funding and equity grants in lagging/remote regions: 320
  participants; Leadership sensitization half-day workshop: 37 Deans of universities; QAA and QE orientation and review:
  1,562 key stakeholders; poverty targeted support to students: 4,000 students' specific inquiries addressed from all
  provinces. The Program has also made progress on gender and climate change. Most of the program results are gender
  tagged and reported. Progress on gender, safeguards and climate change are largely on track. Climate agenda is included
  in almost all DLIs/DLRs. UGC has also prepared a Higher Education Framework for climate change and disseminated.

  alternative signatories and formally share the authorized signatory letter (ASL) with the Bank by June 15, 2025 to
  facilitate the disbursement by June 2025.
