 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Overall performance: The Program’s formal review carried out in March 2023 noted while the implementation of the PforR components are on

  track, the IPF components lag and will either not be completed prior to program closure date or not utilize the total amount allocated. With this view,

  the program is being assessed for potential restructuring within this Fiscal year. Under the PforR component, most of the works have been procured

  and are showing good construction progress. There has been a change in the leadership roles at the Bridge Branch (BB) Project Coordination Unit

  (PCU) with a new Deputy Director General and Program Coordinator appointed. As expected there has been some delay in the Program activities

  due to the handover process, but the Task Team is satisfied with the determination and involvement of the new leadership and is confident that the

  Program will pick up momentum going forward. The NPC has been providing strong support in the verification of the DLI dossiers for disbursement

  and is satisfied with the overall implementation of the Program.

  Current Program for Results (PforR) status: Since the previous implementation review, the BB PCU made good physical progress under DLI-1

  (major maintenance works), DLI-2 (bridge safety works), and DLI-3 (new bridge works), with the DLI-5 (back-log bridges) target already achieved.

  Although the physical progress made under DLI-2 has progressed well, it is important that the procurement for the remaining bridges be completed

  without delay. Currently, the PCU is on track to complete DLI-2 on time. The DLI-3 (new 2-lane bridges) civil works are implemented under Design-

  Build contracts and all packages are under implementation. The physical progress of the new construction bridges under DLI-3 is satisfactory.

  However, the National Vigilance Centre (NVC) has raised a few non-conformances to be addressed by the BB for ongoing construction works. The

  BB will attend to this by establishing a separate desk in the department to manage all the non-conformances raised. The NVC has started the

  procurement for another 41 bridges to undergo technical auditing. The Standard Design-Build bidding documents (DLI-6) are completed and have

  been submitted to MoPIT for approval. Once MoPIT approves the documents and share it with the Public Procurement Monitoring Office (PPMO),

  DLI-6 can be verified for disbursement. The use of the Bridge Site Monitoring System (BSMS) under DLI-7 is underperforming, although it was

  made mandatory by DOR. The new management is confident that this will be reached.

  Current Investment Project Financing (IPF) status: Moderate progress has been made with regards to the IPF components of the operation

  since the last review. The shortlist for the feasibility study and detailed design for upgrading of a priority section of the East-West-Highway

  from Chanauta to Gaddachauki has been established and the Request for Proposal (RFP) will be published soon. The Expression of Interest (EOI)

  for the Environmental and Social Impact Assessment related to the same road will also be floated soon as the Terms of Reference (TOR) are

  currently under review by the Department of Roads (DOR) for approval. As the time left to Program closure is limited, the BB have proposed to

  bring onboard a project management consultant to ensure that the activities under IPF-1 are completed on time. The various training courses for

  capacity development (IPF-4) were attended by BB staff and more training events are planned. The Design and Advanced Technology Cell (DATC)

  (IPF-6) was formed before, but the BB leadership aspires to have it functional for the remainder of the Program and beyond. The draft Occupational

  Community Health and Safety (OCHS) policy, manual and guideline documents (IPF-7) are still being edited by a professional editor. The DOR

  Legal Team will have to review the OCHS policy. Final comments have been provided on the draft Environmental and Social Management

  Framework (ESMF) (IPF-7) and the Consultant is currently incorporating updates for the final version. The new ESMF will have to be approved by

  MoPIT before it can be considered for replacement of the current version. The Consultant is on-board for the impact evaluation baseline and endline

  survey data collection (IPF-9).

  Comments

  The following are explanations provided for the risks categories that are rated as High and Substantial.

  Political and Governance: Substantial. While the new coalition government, which includes two new parties, can provide stability to drive the

  federalism and the reform program for the next five years, the coalition will remain fragile in the short-term as the seven-party alliance settles. The

  federal system provides opportunities to decentralize development benefits and make service delivery more effective and accountable. The

  pandemic also underscored the role of local governments, especially in deploying social programs. Nevertheless, the risks of political uncertainty,

  jurisdictional overlap between the three tiers of government, and need for more clarity and coherence between policies and devolved powers will

  remain in the coming period. While the extent to which political risks can be mitigated through project-specific measures is limited, the World Bank

  will maintain continuity in policy dialogue with the new government, and project design will include specific actions to build public support through

  broader stakeholder participation, strategic communication, and outreach. Accordingly, the overall political and governance risk and its potential

  impact on the Program activities is rated as substantial. .

  Macroeconomics: Substantial. One major risk is the continuation of import control measures to mitigate pressures on international reserves, which

  could affect growth through lower trade-related tax revenues, depressed private consumption and production, and lower capital expenditures. In

  December 2022, import bans on select goods were lifted, but cash deposit and letter of credit requirements for importation remain in place. A

  second risk is the ongoing war in Ukraine, which could lower travel demand and extend higher global commodity prices in the medium term. While

  the downside risks are significant, Nepal’s strong record of sustained macroeconomic stability during periods of large exogenous shocks

  demonstrates a growing capacity to navigate future macroeconomic risks.. Accordingly, the overall macroeconomic risk and its influence on the

  Program is retained as Substantial.

  Fiduciary: Substantial. The residual fiduciary risk under the Program continues to remain substantial. The risk stems from the potential fraud and

  corruption issues likely to face the Program, which could potentially impede achievement of development objectives. For bridge works, key concerns

  relate to collusion or manipulation of bidding processes, fraudulent claims for payment against quantity items, deficient quality combined with

  inappropriate payments for completed works, and erroneous contract variations. To mitigate these risks, the Program has placed additional

  strengthening measures through the Program Action Plan. These include: (a) verification of Bridge Dossiers and physical inspection of Program

  bridges to trigger disbursements, (b) technical auditing by the NVC and subsequent referral of serious non‐ compliances to Nepal’s Commission for

  the Investigation of Abuse of Authority, (c) use of the BSMS which offers enhanced means of quality control on remote worksites.

  Environment and Social: Substantial. The residual risk continues to remain Substantial. There are various potential E&S risks associated with the

  Program such as, vulnerability of works to natural disasters and climate change, potential exposure to child labor, and labor influx including GBV and

  risk of SEA. To mitigate these risks, the Program Actions Plans has been devised with the multi‐tiered approach, that is being followed by the

  implementing agency, as a part of the legal covenants, while implementing the Program. The key implementation support plan also includes a pillar

  on labor risk management focus on monitoring enforcement of contract provisions relating to labor camps, workers’ conduct, prohibition of child

  labor, and access to resources for communities that may be affected.

  Overall: Substantial. Considering the ratings of various risks outlined above, the overall residual risks remains Substantial.
