 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Following project restructuring in October 2018, both components are under full implementation. A number of milestones were achieved in the

  current ISR period. The GCIC moved into new facilities in downtown Accra, in agreement with Ashesi University, to be closer to its clients. The

  GCIC also graduated its fourth cohort of entrepreneurs and brought in its fifth cohort with 30 entrepreneurs, almost doubling the number in prior

  cohorts. The GCVF investment fund was launched after a 2 year delay and related restructuring of the component and is operating in the market as

  Wangara Green Ventures. Its first two deals, in the solar and wastewater sectors, have been approved by its investment committee.

  Due to the strong performance of the GCIC and the launch of the GCVF, the project is exceeding expectations on most of the results indicators in

  the results framework, particularly the number of households reached with climate friendly products, over 220,000 since the project was launched

  (against an original target of 20,000). Beyond its core incubation business, the GCIC is reaching a large number of green economy stakeholders in

  Ghana from policy makers, investors, technology providers, and entrepreneurship organizations. The reach of the GCIC was highlighted during the

  June 2019 Incubating Innovation Symposium with close to 200 green economy stakeholders participating.

  However, one PDO level indicator that tracks the growth in revenues of businesses is not meeting expectations. This largely reflects the 2

  year delayed implementation of the GCVF, component 2. Though initial GCVF investments are now being made, it is uncertain if this result will be

  met within the project time frame.

  Two aspects of the project will be the main focus of project implementation support during the remaining 7 months. First, sustainability of the GCIC

  after the Bank grant ends in June 2020 is an important measure of success. The GCIC is implementing a strong sustainability strategy and

  fundraising effort, with a full time GCIC consultant in place to lead this effort. The Bank and government have agreed in December 2019 to offer

  their support to the GCIC in achieving its fundraising and sustainability ambitions. Second, the GCVF's success will depend on it growing the overall

  size of its fund beyond the Bank grant. The GCVF must successfully raise funds to match the Bank grant to receive the final grant disbursement.

  Achieving this will ensure the GCVF has reached sufficient size to operate sustainably. The Bank is providing experts to assist the GCVF in meeting

  this fundraising requirement.

  GCIC (Component 1):

  Pipeline: The GCIC's enhanced efforts to improve its pipeline are paying off. Cohort 5 is the largest cohort with 30 entrepreneurs/businesses and

  the strongest based on screening results. Over 100 applications have been received for cohort 6. The GCIC is considering how to bring in a 6th

  cohort while funding is only secured at this point through June 2020. A 6 month (rather than a year long) program is being considered.

  Disbursements: The GCIC component is at 83% disbursement and is on track to disburse the full $8.5m RE grant by project closing.

  Staffing: A critical staff member, the Entrepreneurship Director, left the GCIC during the ISR period. This loss has been successfully mitigated

  through recruitment of consultants. The position will be replaced full time once additional financing has been secured for the GCIC beyond June

  2020. A total of 6 Portfolio Managers are now working with GCIC businesses.

  Sustainability: Within the past 6 months, the GCIC has taken steps towards ensuring sustainability of the GCIC after the World Bank grant period.

  An experienced consultant is working full time on fundraising and is collaborating with the Ashesi University Foundation on strategy. Positive

  interest has been identified from bilateral donors, foundations, and corporates.

  GCVF (Component 2):

  Fund Launch: The GCVF was formally launched in June 2019 and operates in the market as Wangara Green Ventures.

  Team and Governance: The GCVF is fully staffed with investment team and back office. Its Steering Committee, Board, and Investment Committee

  are all in place and active. A number of advisors are hired on fund raising, investment structuring, and reporting/systems.

  Disbursements: The GCVF component is at 58% disbursement of the $3.2m RE grant.

  Investments: The GCVF is approaching first investments following screening of over 190 possible deals. The GCVF has also established a referral

  program to provide potential investees with investment readiness training through partnerships with the GCIC and innoHub.
