 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The US$35 million Credit to finance the Public Sector Reform for Results Project (PSRRP) was approved by the Board of Executive Directors of the

  World Bank on October 23, 2018. The project was declared effective on May 8, 2019. The PSRRP supports Ghana’s National Public Sector Reform

  Strategy, 2018-2023. It helps 16 agencies improve efficiency and accountability in delivery of key public services to citizens and firms, such as

  issuance and renewal of passports, vehicle registration and environmental permits, and registration of births and deaths. To incentivize these

  sixteen entities to improve, disbursement-linked indicators (DLIs) will reward entities which achieve agreed results and improve public

  accountability. Implementation support will be provided by the World Bank to strengthen the entities’ capacity to achieve the agreed results.

  The project emphasizes public accountability and reporting, including through a Project Steering Committee (PStC). The PStC, among other things,

  is meant to: (i) review PSRRP implementation progress and achievement of results; (ii) hold semi-annual public meetings where the sixteen entities

  will each submit a report on their progress and results, and their implementation plans for the next six months; and (iii) publish each such written

  report, along with a video recording of the meeting, on the website of the Office of Senior Minister (OSM). The first meeting of the fully constituted

  PtSC (with representatives of three civil society/non-government organizations or CSOs) was held in December 2020. Public PStC meetings are

  expected to be scheduled in 2021 by the OSM. Given the COVID19 situation, the public meetings are intended to be held in safe online format, with

  wide advance public notice - hopefully enabling large media and public participation in the interest of transparency and public accountability.

  The OSM under the Office of the President (OP) implements and manages the project.

  The project has disbursed $4.35 million as of February 15, 2020. Implementation has been slow. The disclosed project results show that not much

  has yet been achieved on the results front - mainly due to numerous project activities being delayed due to procurement capacity challenges, the

  COVID19 pandemic and its effect on the operation of government entities.

  A virtual World Bank Implementation Support-cum-Supervision Mission was conducted during June-July 2020. There has been a change of

  government structure, and the Mid-Term Review mission is expected to take place in April/May 2021 or as soon as the new government is formed.

  A key focus in the short term is to assist the OSM to implement existing activities that can enable service delivery entities to provide selected public

  services in a safe and contactless manner and supplement the government's COVID19 response, and to restructure and adjust the project activities

  and implementation arrangements reflecting the changes on the ground.

 Overall Comments PDO



  As of February 23, 2020 no PDO indicators have been achieved. This is mainly because the PMU still does not (a) have the capacity to either

  rapidly contract technical assistance for Selected Entities to make progress on achieving the PDO indicators, or to provide advice to such

  entities when the entities (e.g. DVLA, OHCS, and CHRAJ) themselves possess the technical capacity to make progress on their own; and

  (b) possess the technical capacity to regularly collect, report and publish data on indicators even when such entities have made significant

  progress in achieving their own sub-indicators which contribute to an overall PDO indicator such as the last PDO indicator. In addition, the

  project's Quarterly Progress Reports prepared by the PMU still do not contain actual data and any analysis of the extent of results achievement

  or does not identify the issues for the delays with results achievement. The results data come from Aide Memoire July 2020.
