PROGRAM CONTEXT AND DEVELOPMENT OBJECTIVES
A. CONTEXT AT APPRAISAL
Program Overview
The First and Second Viet Nam inclusive and sustainable recovery Development Policy Financing (DPF) operations
supported the Government of Viet Nam’s (GoV) effort to promote and sustain a more inclusive, green and digitalfriendly economic recovery. DPF1 (176717) for US$204.5 million of International Development Association (IDA)
Financing, was approved on June 28, 2021, and closed on December 15, 2022, which was its original closing date.
DPF2 (P181146) for US$260.5 million of IDA financing was approved on June 28, 2023, and closed on November 30,
2024, also the original closing date. A two-operation series was prepared, although given budgetary rules, the GoV
initially committed to a single operation that they could directly associate with the expenditure program related to
its 2021 COVID-response plan. In Viet Nam foreign financing can only be secured if associated with an identified
expenditure program or project. Subsequently, the GoV requested the second operation (DPF2) to help sustain and
deepen its reform agenda and to absorb available IDA funds that were about to expire. The program development
objective (PDO) was to support the Government of Viet Nam’s effort to promote and sustain a more inclusive, green
and digital-friendly economic recovery.
Overall and Macroeconomic Context
The COVID-19 crisis affected the Vietnamese economy, although the Vietnamese authorities reacted swiftly and
decisively to control the spread of the virus with a combination of early national lockdown measures, stringent
border and mobility restrictions, targeted testing and tracking and quarantining, as well as innovative
information campaigns. After a 3-week shutdown in April 2020, the domestic economy opened up in May 2020,
while international borders remained closed. This opening helped Viet Nam to be one of the few economies in the
world that experienced positive growth in 2020. The GDP grew by 2.9 percent due to exceptional resilience and
agility in Viet Nam’s export sector, although this growth performance was less than half of the average 6.8 percent
growth registered during the pre-COVID-19 period. At the same time, a relatively large number of households
continued to suffer from the crisis. About 40 percent of households that reported lower income than before the
COVID-19 crisis were expected to cut or postpone their plans to start new construction or a business, or to purchase
a motor vehicle. The poverty rate stood at 5.9 percent in 2020, using a lower middle-income international poverty
line (US$3.2/day 2011 purchasing power parity-PPP). While national poverty rates did not increase due to the
COVID-19 shock, preliminary analysis by the World Bank suggested that the pandemic set back poverty reduction by
one to two years. Annex 7 provides selected macroeconomic indicators.
Sector Context
The COVID pandemic exposed weaknesses in the GoV’s mechanism to support affected businesses and at the
same time offered opportunities to strengthen digitalization of government services and green growth. The
Systematic Country Diagnostic (SCD) of 2021 and the Taking Stock – No Time to waste (2022) identified these
challenges and opportunities which are summarized here.

Pillar 1: Promote and Sustain Inclusive Economic Recovery
A business survey conducted by the VCCI in late 2020 confirmed that about three-quarters of businesses were
negatively affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. Half of businesses faced cash flow constraints, independent of their
size and location.1 Deferral of business taxes for cashflow purposes and administrative burden for taxpayers could
offer needed relief both during and post COVID-19 crisis.
Addressing high poverty rates among ethnic minorities was one of the main development challenges in Viet Nam.
Ethnic minorities constituted 15 percent of the population but more than 86 percent of the poor (based on the
General Statistics Office-World Bank monetary poverty line) in 2018.2 A multi-faceted program of health, education
and livelihood investments was needed to target this hard-core poverty.
Many in the population were isolated from the financial sector with the Vietnamese economy heavily dependent
on cash. According to the 2017 Global Findex survey, ownership of banking accounts in Viet Nam was below the
regional average: about 24 percent of adults in Viet Nam had a debit card in 2017, compared with 43 percent in East
Asia and Pacific countries overall. The government decided to prioritize mobile money particularly in remote areas
along with measures to ensure access to financial services. This required establishing the criteria for participating
enterprises, customer registration requirements, and eligible services for demonstrating the implementation of a
nationwide mobile money system. Advances in digitalization of payments would also have benefits in the
administration of social assistance payments
The participation of women in the labor market was relatively high in Viet Nam (about 70 percent), but
approximately 10 percentage points lower than for men. Inadequate use of and access to quality childcare services
was hindering gender equality in the Vietnamese labor market.3,4 The government viewed subsidized nursery and
pre-school, especially in areas like industrial parks where there is a high concentration of women workers, as a way
to overcome this constraint to women’s employment.
Pillar 2: Promote and Sustain Green and Digital Friendly Economic Recovery
The government viewed Free trade Agreements (FTAs) as a way to diversify the economy and upgrade to
international best practices among other things developing a greener economy. Ratification of the EU-Viet Nam
Free Trade Agreement (EVFTA)-Legislation was needed to accommodate the GoV’s plans to expand FTAs, by
determining the scope of its application as a way to stimulate economic growth. FTAs also offered an opportunity to
apply green principles in procurement and to meet higher international standards.

The Government of Viet Nam had been stepping up efforts on digital government reforms to make government
more efficient, transparent and inclusive. These efforts include the simplification of administrative procedures and
providing public services to citizens and firms via electronic means. The aim of the strategy is to enhance public
service delivery and lower transaction costs for the service users. The regulation on collection, management, and
sharing of data among government agencies is also an important legal foundation for a digital economy. The legal
foundation is necessary to support government agencies’ use of the large amount of government data that are
already collected, improving, in turn, policy decision-making and monitoring. E-procurement was a key part of the
government’s overall digitalization plan.
With ambitious growth plans and the desire to meet climate change goals, there was a need to accelerate
renewable energy generation capacity. Viet Nam was highly successful in providing universal electrification (99
percent in 2020) and in keeping pace with double-digit growth in electricity demand in the previous two decades
(installed capacity increased from 5 GW in 2000 to 58 GW in 2020). However, coal became the dominant source of
electricity generation, with installed capacity increasing fourfold (from 5 GW in 2010 to 20 GW in 2020). With the
international commitments made by the authorities to transition away from coal, it was critical to promote
renewable energy capacity and ensure that the right price signals were sent to consumers of electricity. The Socioeconomic Development Strategy (SEDS) 2021-2030 emphasized climate resilience and promoting green growth,
supported by a comprehensive reform agenda for greening growth and highlighting the importance of the adoption
of market-based tools, transparency, and sharing information.
Rationale for Bank Involvement
The planned reforms reflected the long standing and broad-based partnership between Viet Nam and the WBG
which was, in large part, demonstrated during the preparation of Viet Nam’s 2021-2030 SEDS, a strategy that
aims to transform its economy into a modern, sustainable and high-income economy by 2045. The WBG
contributed to various background studies, including the joint Viet Nam 2035 Report. The COVID-19 Policy Note
series delivered during the first half of 2020 helped the authorities design and implement their response to the
pandemic. Several technical assistance programs were ongoing, partly funded with the support of development
partners, in the areas of tax policy and administration, social protection, financial sector, trade, e-government, and
energy. The authorities were interested to secure budget support initially to finance their COVID-19 socio-economic
recovery programs in 2021 and again in 2023 to support the two-year (2022-23) recovery program adopted by the
National Assembly (Resolution 43, January 2022), especially as they could secure these funds in IDA terms, given
available IDA funds from projects closures and/or cancelations that could be recommitted by the end of FY21 and
again in FY23. The canceled IDA funds were due in large part to the restructuring of IDA IPFs portfolio, which led to
removal of project activities and unused funds.

digital-friendly economic recovery.

COVID-19 crisis on businesses and people. Beyond immediate financial support, the series promoted further

Pillar 2 promoted sustainable recovery through digitalization and green growth. The adoption of several regional

digitalization process of the government and the transition toward a low carbon economy through green
procurement and promotion of solar energy capacity.

B. SIGNIFICANT CHANGES DURING IMPLEMENTATION
Context for DPF2
The macro environment improved quickly after COVID-19 but was subject to a series of global shocks. These
shocks included the oil price increase in 2022 and US increase in interest rates to stave off inflationary pressures
thereafter. GDP growth rebounded from 2.6 percent to 8.5 percent in 2022 but moderated to 5.1 percent in 2023.
Inflation ticked up from 1.8 percent during COVID in 2021 to 3.2 and 3.3 percent in 2022 and 2023, respectively.
While official unemployment fell from 2.4 percent in 2021 and remained in the 1.5-1.7 percent range during 202223, underemployment rose and labor participation fell.
A similar situation as with DPF1 emerged prior to DPF2 with a short window to go through WB internal
processes, with Government looking to finance its 2022-2023 economic recovery program and again facing the
risk of loss of IDA resources. The GoV decided after internal deliberation to cancel IDA-funded IPFs. The freed-up
IDA resources would have been lost to Viet Nam had they not been repurposed by the government. The extensive,
long-term engagement already in place between the WB and the GoV made it possible to deliver a substantively
strong DPF2 within an extremely compressed time schedule while respecting all World Bank internal procedures.
Given the global and domestic economic and policy developments in 2021-2023, the DPF2 reform agenda, which
had been designed from the beginning of the series preparation was updated and enhanced and was approved
using the available IDA funding. As stated above, these IDA resources helped finance the GoV recovery program
defined by Decree 43.
Revised Program Development Objectives (PDOs)
The original PDO was revised slightly to eliminate the phrase “in the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic,”
primarily to reflect the passage of time since the pandemic had ended and socio-economic circumstances had
changed.

Rating: Satisfactory

themes: improved government efficiency through digitalization, financial inclusion, greener practices for
development and general economic recovery. The series supported enabling legislation and decrees to ease

agenda. The design emphasized making government more efficient and transparent through digitalization
which permeated the program, including e-invoices for taxes, e-procurement, payment of social assistance,
promoting nation-wide mobile money, online government services and digitalization of the public
administration, and inter ministerial sharing of digital data and increasing renewable energy (RE) generation
and cost recovery for electricity. The design of the series embodied deep, mutually reinforcing actions across
the four themes, consistent with Vietnamese national and WB corporate priorities. Table 3 summarizes the
theory of change.

Childcare support for economic inclusion for women. Women’s opportunities for and choices to engage
and stay in the labor market were promoted through improved access to childcare services. As Viet Nam
builds new factories and industrial parks (IPs) in semi-urban and rural areas, the supply of childcare facilities
is not keeping pace with the influx of workers and families migrating to take factory jobs. Use of childcare

Mobile money to promote inclusion. This reform was highly relevant to financial inclusion because it
provided the entry point in the use of financial services by those previously excluded, including for the
purpose of receiving remittances or social benefits. Digital payment channels are a key driver for financial
inclusion as they empower those lacking access to banking services to access other formal financial services.
Developing an inclusive digital payments ecosystem is essential for providing basic financial services to the
excluded or underserved. The development of mobile money is expected to help unbanked households,
which are disproportionally represented in poor and aged groups of the population. Mobile money also fits
well with the government’s digitalization agenda.
Microinsurance to promote women’s economic inclusion. The program aimed to introduce microinsurance products to manage risks faced by the most vulnerable segment of the population, including
women. Micro insurance is relevant to credit expansion because it gives creditors some reassurance that
they will be repaid in the case of the debtor’s death or injury. However, the reform (DPF2 PA3) was not
adequate by itself to achieve that objective as the initial trial period administered by the Women’s Union
was not scaled up by the Government. Thus, relevance is modest.

monitoring.

Relevance of indicators. Most indicators were intermediate in nature measuring the degree of coverage of

PDO 2: Promote and sustain digital friendly economic recovery
The indicators associated with digitalization to improve coverage and efficiency of service delivery were
mostly measuring the degree of coverage of the service or reform and directly attributable to the relevant
prior actions.
The program supported the introduction of online filing and online payment for the CIT and VAT and updated
the information technology infrastructure of its tax administration through the adoption of e-invoices. Einvoices simplify VAT filing, especially by saving compliance time and administrative costs. The tax
administration had been piloting VAT e-invoices since 2015, and DPF2 support helped achieve e-invoices for all
taxpayers nationwide, thus meeting the RI1.2 target of 100% of all filers, and greatly exceeding the DPF1 target
of 10%. DPF2 PA1
Replacing cash payments with direct electronic deposits through the digitilization of the social assistance
database and linking it to the National Population Database (with unique ID numbers for all citizens) has
made rapid progress in lowering transaction costs for administration and beneficiaries and reduced the
potential for fraud. With the unique IDs payments to duplicate and non-existent individuals are eliminated. The
Ministry of Labor and Social Affairs (MoLISA) was mandated to digitalize the social assistance database and link
to the social national ID database managed by the Ministry of Public Security. With the July 2025 government
reorganization, the responsibility now falls under the Ministry of Health. The social assistance database allows
people to register online and receive benefits via bank accounts. RI4.2 was the percentage of social assistance
beneficiaries who receive e- payments and the target of 20% was greatly exceeded with a 50% achievement (1.9
million out of 3.8 million social assistance beneficiaries). DPF2 PA4
E-government reforms led to a large and rapid increase of digitalization of government services, while also
improving the quality of service delivery (DPF1 PA6, DPF2 PA7). RI7.1 measured the percentage of government
services available online which increased from 30% (2020) to 93%, exceeding the 85% target. At the same time,
a composite measure of quality of service delivery, RI7.2 increased from 55.6% to 76.5 %, again exceeding the
target (65%). To build on the e-government benefits, the series also supported rules for sharing digital data
across ministries at all levels of government (DPF1 PA7). This PA allowed consumers of government information
to make better decisions regarding for example, choosing schools, going to the right hospitals that can treat
their needs most effectively and many other benefits from information sharing. RI9 measured the progress of
this measure through ‘ministries and provincial governments connected to the National Government Service
Platform (NGSP), with a target of all 63 provinces connected, which was achieved. The number of provinces has
since been consolidated to 34 which are all connected to the NGSP.
The series supported major advances in digitalization and transparency through the expansion of eprocurement (DPF2 PA8). The value of contracts procured using e-procurement over total competitively
procured contracts value reached 95% compared to a baseline of only 25% and a target of 90 percent, thus
achieving the target. Note that the denominator excludes some security-based contracts that by law cannot be

PDO 3: Promote and Sustain Inclusive Economic Recovery
The GoV achieved significant progress in raising the status of zone 3 communes, thus contributing to
economic inclusion of deeply disadvantaged segments of the population. The Gov defined a list of eligible
communes and villages in the ethnic minority and mountainous areas, categorized into zones 1, 2, and 3 based
on multi-dimensional (MdP) indicators measured using communal and village-level data. It has also developed
financing mechanisms to target state budget allocation to implementing agencies and participating localities.
The program supported the formulation and adoption of a financing mechanism for NTP SEDEMA which
addresses the key weaknesses identified in the previous NTPs. The financing mechanism includes funding
formulas for allocating central budget that gives priority to the poorest communes based on the MdP criteria,
subprogram-specific criteria, and the share of the most disadvantaged (zone-3) communes/villages. The funding
formulas included an increase in the average capital investment for zone-3 communes by about 50 percent
compared to the previous period. The funding allocation mechanism in NTP-SEDEMA focused on the poorest
communes and villages in ethnic minority and mountainous regions than their predecessors. The PA was
expected to help communes move out of the extremely disadvantaged category as a result of the policy reform.
Therefore, the result indicator RI2 was defined as the percentage of extremely disadvantaged communes (zone3 communes) moving out of the zone-3 communes’ classification with a target (2024) of 30%. The program
achieved 20%, which given the delay in budget disbursement of two years, is a significant accomplishment, but
is rated as substantially achieved. DPF1 and 2 PA2
The reform for microinsurance was launched and achieved some progress but has been discontinued. The
Government established a legal basis for mutual organizations to offer micro-insurance products targeted for
the poor population. The clear definition and scope of micro-insurance enabled the Government to establish
certain incentive mechanisms targeted at this segment of the industry. The Women’s Union continued to offer
(after the 2014 pilot) micro-insurance products. RI3 measured the number of micro-insurance contracts
administered by mutual organizations with a baseline in 2022 of 0 and a target of 35,000 (2024). The total
achieved was not reported. The main issue with the reform was that there was insufficient financial incentive to
continue issuing the contracts. In addition, the Women’s Union, who was issuing the contracts, dissolved with
no other entities willing to pursue the activity. The concept has not been abandoned and the GoV has been
exploring additional support that will make it attractive to entities to take up the microinsurance vehicle. DPF2

measures proposed by the bidder; and (iii) life cycle costs including operation, maintenance, and disposal

the GSO. Going forward, procurements will need to be tagged in some way so that the PPA can measure the

The series supported government efforts to increase solar energy capacity in Viet Nam though the planning of

distribution networks, which could then not absorb all of the generated RE. The issue was that licenses were
issued at the provincial level and were not carefully thought through. At the same time, private investors
wanted to benefit from the generous FIT, which was several times the average kwh price of electricity. The

C. OVERALL OUTCOME RATING AND JUSTIFICATION
Rating: Moderately Satisfactory
The series achieved an overall moderately satisfactory rating for outcome. Digitization has been an impressive
success and has helped prepare for the major government consolidation that was official on July 1, 2025. The
progress toward the objective of inclusion was also impressive with large swaths of the deeply poor elevated to
the next highest rating for communes. Inclusion was also advanced through addressing women’s key constraint
to seeking employment, affordable childcare. The availability of childcare was also raised which benefits the
labor force, especially female workers. Mobile money was also a important achievement in including the
unbanked into the economy. The experience with micro insurance was below expectations, but the government
is revisiting ways to increase access to credit. The general economic recovery was promoted through the
business reforms and ratification of FTAs. The greening of the government and the energy sector was also
advanced, though it is work in progress and remains challenging as public investment in transmission has lagged
and the government needs to clarify the policy framework on investment and pricing green electricity
production and sales more thoroughly. While greening public procurement will likely be an effective tool to
incentivize behavioral change in private suppliers, there needs to be a better application of green procurement
criteria and better tracking of same to ensure that the reform is being implemented.

A. POVERTY, GENDER AND SOCIAL IMPACTS
The series focused on and was successful in reducing poverty and improving gender inclusion. As documented
above, substantial progress was achieved in reducing poverty in zone 3 communes, reducing transactions costs
for recipients of social benefits, and supporting female inclusion in the economy by subsidizing more and betterPage 21

quality childcare. With respect to electricity tariff increases, most people in poor and near-poor households

B. ENVIRONMENTAL, FORESTS, AND NATURAL RESOURCE ASPECTS
The series achieved significant positive effects toward a greener government and overall economy.
Digitization itself achieves a form of “greening” by reducing people’s need/travel time to appear in person to
access government services and receive and make payments. Support of greening energy production The
quadrupling of solar generation from 4GW to 16.7GW could potentially achieve significant climate benefits by
replacing coal and oil-based consumption.
C. INSTITUTIONAL CHANGE/STRENGTHENING
The series did not focus on institutional capacity building, but the advances in digitization strengthened
institutions’ ability to deliver public services more efficiently. The tie-in of social service payments with the
national identity card allowed institutions to make payments more quickly and with fewer staff. The sharing of
data between agencies enabled more informed decisions by government staff as well as consumers of
government services. Improved delivery of services from digitalization was evidenced by improvements in the
composite quality indicator (RI 7.2). Also, institutions continue to be strengthened as e-invoicing for VAT and eprocurement continue to be used.
D. OTHER UNINTENDED OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS
The advances in digitalization were essential in accommodating the major consolidation of government.
Having more automated processes based on a national ID card greatly simplified the transition to fewer
ministries and provinces and the elimination of the entire district level of local government.

Rating: Satisfactory
Analytic Work and Application of Lessons Learned
The design of the DPO series benefited from the long-standing policy engagement in Viet Nam, along with the
experience of the previous DPO series in Viet Nam and in other countries in the region. The main lessons
were: i) the need to align the reforms with the government priorities and ii) to focus on a few specific but

strategic results. Lesson i) was clearly applied because all of the PAs were based on government decisions and
were part of the core government’s development program. Lesson ii) was not that closely followed as evidenced
the series coverage of a wide range of sectors: technological, fiscal, social, energy and environmental reforms.
Despite the breadth of the series, the actions coalesced around the four themes of general economic recovery,
a greener economy, inclusion and digitalization of government. The result was a two-operation DPF series that
contributed to transformative results that has also enhanced the potential success of the subsequent
government consolidation in the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic.
Risk Assessment and Mitigation
Risk was minimized partly due to the long-standing engagement between the WB and the Vietnamese
government and partly because the series focused on critical PAs in select sectors that supported the
government’s ongoing reform agenda. The success of the PAs was enhanced through WBG support in a number
of ways, including through the active country dialog and especially the long standing and in-depth analytical
and TA engagements including in areas such as women’s employment, digitalization, private sector
development, financial sector, procurement, transport, and energy. With the heavy emphasis on digitalization,
the program needed to account for risks to data security, particularly with the advances in on-line registration
with authentication of the individual’s identity To address risks associated to privacy and data security, all
agencies involved are required to follow the system security requirements (Decree 85/2016/ND-CP) and the
newly issued decree on personal data protection (Decree 12/2023/ND-CP). Another benefit of digitalization is
that rapid enrolment and payment allows for a more timely response to natural disasters and crises.
Fast and Flexible Preparation by the WB Team
The WB team deserves high marks for maintaining a policy dialog that was relevant to national priorities and
responded very quickly when the government decided to move forward with the DPF. The government had
not engaged regularly in DPFs and was pursuing its reform program largely independently of external
borrowing. Government internal deliberations continued until late March in 2021 and in 2023 before the
decision to go ahead with the DPF program. The timing of this decision left a very short timeframe for internal
processing of the operations. However, to ensure high quality, all managerial reviews as well as formal CN and
ROC reviews were undertaken. To complete processing in such a short timeframe, the WB team worked
intensively, conducting final negotiations and obtaining approvals prior to the end-of-FY deadlines.
Supervision and Quality of M&E
The WB team helped maintain an up-to-date results monitoring made necessary adjustments to the RIs and
program triggers. After implementation of DPF1, and the nearly two years until DPF2 approval, there were a
significant number of changes in the context of the operation, including more advanced progress than
anticipated. There were also a number of trigger changes that made the PAs a more precise expression of the
reforms.

RISK TO SUSTAINABILITY OF DEVELOPMENT OUTCOMES
The risk to sustainability to most areas of the reform program is low. The series supported measures that were
deeply embedded in the government’s development strategy. In Viet Nam, reforms usually require legal or
regulatory changes, and implementation of these changes are exercised through a variety of decision or
instruction, of which, the PM decisions have the highest authority. In other words, PM decision represent a
strong instrument for legal and regulatory change. The major portion of the program, digitization of
government services, is a permanent feature now of the government’s way of doing business. One area that
would need additional effort to achieve a positive outcome is the microinsurance vehicle to help improve credit
access. Maintaining gains for Zone 3 communes and promotion of more and higher quality childcare to
accommodate higher female employment will require continued budget allocations that are significant. These
allocations appear to be sustained. Greater energy supply for a low carbon economy will require additional
strategies to encourage private sector investment in solar and other RE as well as government rollout of its
electricity strategy and investment in electricity transmission and distribution.

VI. LESSONS AND NEXT PHASE
A. LESSONS LEARNED
In countries where DPFs are not common, it is advantageous, nevertheless, to have an up to date, ongoing
policy options matrix that can be supported when the opportunity for policy-based lending arises. In the case
of Viet Nam, regular DPFs were not a priority for the government. However, an opportunity arose to support
the government’s reform and recovery program through IDA funding. The WB was able to assemble a
meaningful reform program in areas where there was a deep engagement, supporting female employment and
digitalization of government services, but also in areas where engagement was more at the dialog level, such as
tax deferrals, electricity tariffs and the NTP for ethnic minorities. The WB maintaining a good grasp of
government priorities made it possible to respond quickly to the government’s decision to move forward with
DPF1 and with DPF2. The fast and flexible approach of the WB team allowed responsiveness to client needs in
policy support and financing, while maintaining internal checks and balances ensured quality control.
Close assessment of the realism of government results targets is warranted. As discussed above, there were 3
significant over and underestimations of the initial RIs, resulting in recalibration during DPF 2. While the team’s
concern was to register the most accurate representation of the impact of the PA in alignment with government
vision, the large revisions in RI values highlight the need for a more thoughtful assessment of government goals
and infusion of more realism in the subsequent choice of RIs values. On the other hand, the changes between
percentage based and nominal indicators were based on recommendations by the technical teams or reviewers
(including OPCS) and depended on availability of better/new data.

To be successful, some measures need more than authorizing legislation, in particular, adequate incentives to
sustain the program. In the case of microinsurance the PA supported a pilot, with the expectation that the
fundamental requirements for the sustainability of the measures would be put into place post-pilot period
However, these requirements for entities to issue micro-insurance contracts, profit, did not materialize. As a
result, the program disbanded along with its implementer, the Women’s Union.
When licensing the private sector for government objectives, it is essential that the process for licensing be
adequately vetted and that incentives are not too generous to attract unqualified investors. The FIT was too
generous and encouraged substantial rent-seeking in the provinces. Licenses for private power generators need
to be issued in accordance with technical capacity to absorb the electricity through adequate planning and
investment in electricity transmission, which requires timely and adequate investment in network infrastructure
by EVN and its subsidiaries.
A concerted program of digitalizing government can generate many different types of benefits including
efficiency, transparency, inclusion and environmental. The series achieved efficiency benefits through digital
payments and speed of processing. Inclusion benefits were achieved by reaching remote, disadvantaged
communities through mobile money. Transparency and accountability benefits were realized through eprocurement, but also through social benefits payments linked to a national ID card that eliminated duplicate
payments. Digitilization also promotes a greener economy because of fewer paper-based transactions, and less
travel time to access payments in cash.

B. NEXT PHASE
The DPF series provides a platform for the Bank to continue to engage with the government, especially in the
context of major institutional and policy reforms that are being implemented. The merger of the Ministry of
Finance and the Ministry of Planning and Investment (effective from March 2025) reinforced the role of the new
MOF as the lead agency to prepare DPF operations, including those involving reforms in sectoral policies. A
number of Viet Nam’s Communist Party strategic resolutions, including in areas of science, technology and
digitalization; private sector development; education; energy security; health care; and social protection, also
provide clear directions for policy reforms in the next five years. This presents opportunities for further
engagements with the government should it request direct budget support from the WB.
