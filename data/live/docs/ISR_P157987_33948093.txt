 Project Development Objectives

  Project Development Objective (from Project Appraisal Document)
  The Project aims to improve the capacity of the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) to produce quality core statistics and make them accessible
  in a timely manner to policy makers and the public.

 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  Summary of Project Implementation Progress and Key Issues
  Component A. Improving the Coordination and Management of Statistical Activities
     1. Mechanisms to enhance coordination and reporting on HR practices: The TP has submitted the deliverables on (i) A. 1.1 Report on
        MoUs, (ii) A.1.2 Report on Administrative Data, (iii) A.2.1 Report on Functional Review and (iv) A.2.2 Report on HR Recruitment. Activity
        A.1.1. Report on MoUs is pending. TP, in cooperation with BBS’ custodian (FA and MIS Wing), is responsible for the deliverable of A1.1.
        Thus far, TP has organized a workshop with other institutions and held introductory meetings from which a draft MoU has been prepared.
        Response from the BBS Wings is required to finalize this activity and deliverable. TP has submitted materials for A.2.3 Training on Quality
        Management which is to take place in December 2022.

       2.    Memorandum of Understanding (MoU): TP requires feedback from the BBS Wings regarding information related to demands from
             different government ministries and institutions on data sharing. The feedback process has been slow and delayed the drafting of the
             MoUs. A meeting was held on September 26, 2022, between Dr. Shahnaz Arefin, NDC, Secretary, Statistics, and Informatics Division
             (SID), Mr. Md. Matiar Rahman, Director General, BBS, and the heads of different BBS wings to discuss issues regarding the MoUs. Mr.
             Md. Matiar Rahman, Director General, BBS acknowledged that the MoUs, being an effective tool, would help facilitate the access to this
             information across institutions producing data. The meeting provided renewed momentum on the matter, and PIU informed the mission
             that a response will be provided to the TP within two weeks.
        3. Trainings: TP has submitted the materials for the Training on Quality Management. The mission noted that BBS needs to respond with
             available dates for the required trainings. TP has additionally suggested merging similar local trainings to make this process efficient and
             BBS agreed with this suggestion.
  Component B. Developing Human Resources and ICT Infrastructure to Produce and Manage Data
        1. Investing in core skills and competencies: This component partly focuses on the development of Human Resources (HR). It includes
             the drafting of a training policy as well as the full “training engineering cycle” that starts from the identification of training needs and supply,
             the design, and, finally, the implementation of a training program. The objective was to develop human resources by investing in core skills
             and competencies. The mission was brought to speed on the trainings conducted since the March 2022 based on the Training needs
             assessment and inventory of statistical trainings. These include:
                   a. Training on Introduction to Time Series Analysis, STATA and R Software were conducted for specialist staff.
                   b. Quantum Index of Industrial Production (QIIP) and Producer Price Index (PPI) training held at the BBS and field offices.
                   c. TP provided training on the Fundamental on Price Statistics, Health Statistics, and Labor Statistics to enhance the capacity
                        of participants to contribute to a meaningful development of these Statistics, in particular the collection of appropriate and
                        accurate data in line with international standards and the interpretation of statistics obtained considering national policies and
                        circumstances.
                   d. A three-day Refresher Training for enumerators was held on HIES and QLFS surveys.
                   e. Two batches of 69 officials were provided training on Sampling Technique and Practice by TP.
                   f.   A batch of 20 officials were provided training on Statistical Methods for Estimation and Analysis by TP.
                   g. Training on Survey Management Design and Operations was provided to 76 BBS officials.
                   h. Basic training was provided to 500 staff on Computer Literacy and another 1495 officials were trained over two rounds on
                        Basic Agriculture Training by PIU.
  2. Investments in ICT infrastructure to support data collection and management: Drafts of the ICT Plan and ICT Roadmap has been prepared
  by TP and is under review. The ICT Roadmap will determine the shortlist of ICT equipment for procurement which represents a large part of the
  remaining project procurement activities. The delay in finalizing the ICT Plan and ICT Roadmap is causing further delays in the completion of the
  project procurement activities. The project financing agreement states that all project goods are to be procured and purchased within the project
  period (by June 2023). The procurement process itself is time intensive as it requires getting the correct product specifications, installing and
  setting-up equipment, commissioning and receiving training from independent suppliers. Further delays are expected due to a global shortage of
  microchips due to hampered production during the pandemic. An additional challenge once the equipment list is prepared would be to consult the
  recommended items with the various government ministries and departments for their acceptance and ownership. As the international training
  component may be challenging to implement, PIU has requested a reallocation of funds from Component D (international training) to Component C
  which includes ICT procurement.
  3. Developing the website: The website has been set up and functional on the internet web space. Website design has been done following
  international best practices in the website framing and information display. TP has received feedback from the SID Secretary and BBS Validation
  Group and has taken everything into account in the revision process that is still pending. The main feedback from these meeting was to restructure
  the website including the different BBS Wings. PIU will arrange a management meeting to discuss and agree on the key changes that BBS would
  prefer for the website. TP will organize the website maintenance and update training with relevant experts. TP will also undertake server hosting
  during development and hand it over to BBS once all changes have been incorporated and approved.
  Component C. Improving the Coverage and Quality of Core Statistics Required for Policy Making
        1. Key surveys completed: BBS completed the 2022 Population and Housing Census (PHC) using Computer-Assisted Personal
             Interviewing (CAPI) in June 2022. The preliminary report was completed within a month (July 2022) and published on the BBS website.
             Further, the PIU was able to conduct the first ever User Satisfaction Survey (USS). The report is currently under review for publication.
        2. Progress in implementation of the 2022 Labor Force Survey: Quarterly Labor Force Survey (QLFS) by the Industry and Labor Wing of
             the BBS started in January 2022. TP expert on labor statistics provided technical support in terms of developing and reviewing the
             questionnaire and methodology. The target is to complete the Quarterly LFS survey series over a 3-year period (2023-2024). Data
             collection in the first year will be completed by December 31, 2022. The sampling frame consists of 1,284 Primary Sampling Unites
             (PSUs)/Enumerator Areas (EAs) with 107 data enumerators. At present, the third quarter LFS data collection is ongoing. The NSDS
             project further supported a Refresher Training for the enumerators, held August 25-27, 2022, mainly to (i) review survey materials, (ii)
             create space for discussion of the issues and challenges at the field, and (iii) provide advice on the mitigation steps and to improve the
             quality of data collected and supervision. Once data collected is completed in December 2022, Industry and Labor Wing will start on data
             cleaning, analysis and LFS report writing. As part of the survey supervision and preparation for the annual survey report writing, the LFS
             project team has developed and used data monitoring system via CAPI for data consistency checks, and has prepared report outline, draft
             sample weights, and list of key indicators. key Indicators to be covered among others, include employment, unemployment, migration, and
             disaggregated by age groups and gender. The team however requires TP’s expertise to code the industrial and occupational data. In the
             previous year’s publication, a 4 to 5 digit industrial and 6-digit occupational coding system was developed for the Annual Report, however
             the coding system cannot be applied at the field level and must be done centrally.
        3. Progress in implementation of the 2022 Household Income and Expenditure Survey (HIES): The Household Income and
             Expenditure Survey (HIES) started its 14th Term of data collection from September 21. Director General of BBS has sent official

      4. Improving Coverage and Quality of Core Statistics: The project continues to support improvement of the Core Statistics in line with
           international standards. For monitoring project objectives, the project focuses on 8 domains of core statistics, including; (i) Health Statistics
           (Health and Morbidity Survey), (ii) Labor Statistics (Labor Force Survey), (iii) Industry Statistics (Survey of manufacturing Industries), (iv)
           Price Statistics (Consumer Price Index), (v) National Accounts (Quarterly National Accounts), (vi) Agricultural Statistics (Crop Statistics),
           (vii) Agriculture Statistics (Fisheries Statistics) and (viii) Poverty/Welfare Statistics (Household Income and Expenditure Survey). The PIU
           also monitors release of survey reports and data according to the release calendar.
  Component D. Promoting and Strengthening Access to and the Use of Official Statistics
      1. Documenting statistical activities and providing better access to data: TP experts continue to provide technical support for
           documentation, microdata anonymization and making data accessible for further research and analysis. The review and finalization of the
           several deliverables have been done, including survey documentation, data visualization, preparation of codes and syntax and micro data
           anonymization. The new BBS website was also presented to the BBS and SID management.
  Component E. Project Management
      1. Revision and approval of the Technical Assistance Project Proposal (TAPP) and the TP Contract: After extension of the project
           closing date to June 30, 2023, two of the key pending tasks were the revision and approval of TAPP and the adjustment, extension and
           signing of the TP contract to allow for the completion of activities. The closing date for both, TAPP and TP contract was extended to
           February 28, 2022, which still differs from the NSDS project closing date of June 30, 2023.
      2. Progress in TP activities. The progress in implementation of the TP contract is satisfactory, having successfully completed more than a
           half of the deliverables. The mission learned that the TP has delivered 20 final and draft deliverables out of the 24 total project deliverables
           across the four main project components (Component A, B, C and D) and has carried out local basic and advance trainings (7 trainings
           and 170 staff trained). The implementation of the short-term intermediate level trainings is currently on hold due to government restriction
           on international travels by civil servants.
  Procurement.
      1. Procurement Performance, Planning and Processes: The mission reviewed the procurement plan and noted that all actual dates for
           completed items have been populated, which giving a real picture of the procurements undertaken. The mission suggested that the PIU
           works with the TP on the remaining high value and essential ICT equipment to avoid further delays based on the preliminary ICT Plan and
           ICT Roadmap.
      2. Rating: Following the review and discussions above, the overall Procurement performance for the project is rated Satisfactory.
  Financial Management: The financial management performance is rated Satisfactory.
  Next Mission: The next mission is planned for March 2023.
