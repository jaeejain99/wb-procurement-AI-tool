I. PROGRAM CONTEXT AND DEVELOPMENT OBJECTIVES



A. CONTEXT AT APPRAISAL





1. At the time of appraisal, the political context in Nepal remained stable, supported by a sound macroeconomic

framework. Despite several shocks in the past (including unstable governments, earthquakes, and trade disruptions,

Nepal’s macroeconomic fundamentals were sound as evidenced by solid revenue growth, a low debt -to-Gross

Domestic Product (GDP) ratio, a stable financial sector and sufficient reserves. Although risks persisted due to high

fiscal challenges from federalism and external deficits, reliance on remittances, global economic uncertainties, and

vulnerability to climate and disaster shocks, the government remained committed to macro-fiscal prudence by

adopting fiscal discipline during the transition to federalism, undertaking reforms on climate and disaster risk

management, and committing to increasing revenues to finance the higher cost of federalism, undertaking a

spending review to contain expenditure and improve their efficiencies through strengthened public financial

management, and continued commitment to maintain the peg with the Indian Rupee.



2. Nepal faced significant risks from natural disasters and public health threats due to its geographic and climatic

conditions. The country was (and continues to be) highly vulnerable to earthquakes, floods, landslides, droughts, and

disease outbreaks, ranking among the most at-risk nations worldwide. Recent events, including the devastating 2015

earthquakes and severe flooding, had caused extensive loss of life, displacement, and damage to infrastructure,

exposing weaknesses in building practices and emergency preparedness. The 2015 earthquakes alone resulted in

nearly 8,800 deaths, over 22,000 injuries, and approximately US$7 billion in recovery needs, equivalent to one-third

of the nation’s GDP at the time. The 2017 floods killed over 150 people and around 460,000 people were displaced.

Incessant rainfall in July 2019 caused 92 landslides and 83 instances of flooding, killed over 100 people, and

temporarily displaced over 16,520 households (approximately 80,000 people) in Central and Eastern Nepal.



3. Government resources remained insufficient to address the full scale of post-disaster recovery and health

sector demands despite the annual allocation of contingency funds. Ministries often had to reallocate development

budgets to cover relief and reconstruction efforts during emergencies. This underscored the urgent need for Nepal

to enhance emergency preparedness and financial protection by securing reliable financial instruments—such as the

Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option (Cat DDO) operation—to enable timely response and sustainable recovery

following disasters or health crises.



4. At appraisal, Nepal’s disaster risk management and climate adaptation framework had advanced significantly

through robust legislation, policy reform, and institutional strengthening. The 2017 Disaster Risk Reduction and

Management Act marked a shift from reactive to proactive disaster management, creating national and local

authorities for coordinated action. This Act aligned with the 2009 National Strategy for Disaster Risk Management

and the international Sendai Framework, and integrated climate change adaptation in national planning. Ongoing

efforts included the Nepal National Building Code, National Disaster Response Framework (NDRF), and the

establishment of emergency operations centers. The government institutionalized climate change adaptation

through policies, budget codes, and strategic plans such as the National Adaptation Programme of Action and

Nationally Determined Contributions under the Paris Agreement. Health emergency preparedness was prioritized

through national strategies and the rollout of early warning systems. These comprehensive reforms provided a

foundation for effective disaster and climate resilience programming.

5. In addition to the Constitution of Nepal 2015, the Local Government Operation Act 2017, and the DRRM Act

2017, key legal and policy documents related to DRRM included the Building Act 1998, Nepal National Building Code

developed in 1994 and enacted in 2004, the National Strategy for Disaster Risk Management 2009, National Disaster

Risk Reduction Strategic Plan of Action 2018-2030, and NDRF 2012. Annual disaster drills initiated in 2009 and further

streamlined through NDRF, as well as the establishment of the National Emergency Operations Center in 2010

enhanced preparedness and coordination. Since 2010, disaster, environment, and climate change have been

systematically integrated into national periodic development plans. The Climate Change Policy 2011 and the

introduction of the Climate Change Budget Code in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2013 strengthened climate-responsive

governance. Health emergency preparedness was also embedded within the Nepal Health Sector Strategy (2015–

2020), with Early Warning and Reporting Systems and Health Emergency Operations Centers further strengthening

surveillance and response capacities for large-scale disease outbreaks.



6. The operation built on the Bank’s past and ongoing engagement in Nepal focusing on strengthening institutional

capacity. For example, while the Earthquake Housing Reconstruction Project (EHRP) supported the post 2015

earthquake housing reconstruction, the Cat DDO supported the strengthening of regulations such as the Disaster

Risk Reduction and Management Regulation to establish the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management

Authority (NDRRMA) and update of the National Building Codes (NBCs) to incorporate seismic resilience. The Building

Resilience to Climate Related Hazard Project (US$31 million, 2013 – 2018) aimed to modernize and automate the

hydro-meteorological observation network; the EHRP (IDA US$700 million, 2015 – 2022) contributed to the

reconstruction of disaster resilient houses in earthquake affected areas. In addition, the Regional Climate Adaptation

and Resilience for South Asia Project (P171054) aimed to contribute to an enabling environment for climate resilience

policies and investments in Water, Agriculture and Transport sectors in Nepal, Bangladesh and Pakistan.



7. The operation was consistent with the WBG Country Partnership Framework (CPF) FY 19–23 for Nepal discussed

by the Board of Executive Directors on August 7, 2018 (Report No. 121029-NP) and extended to FY24 (by the

Performance and Learning Review dated January 17, 2022). The operation contributed to the CPF’s Objective 3.3:

Increased resilience to health shocks, natural disasters, and climate change, with the stated focus of securing

emergency financing in the immediate aftermath of climate-induced extreme events and potential pandemics. The

2018 Systematic Country Diagnostic (SCD) identified several challenges, including exposure to external demand

shocks from natural disasters and limited macroeconomic and fiscal space to deal with an external shock. The SCD

also recognized that improving preparedness for disasters and climate change would have a medium- to long-term

impact on reducing macro fiscal risk.



8. The Cat DDO provided a line of contingent financing to support the government’s ongoing efforts to increase

its capacity to absorb potential fiscal impacts from adverse events. The Cat DDO allowed the Government of Nepal

(GoN) to secure prompt access to financing in the event of a natural catastrophe, building a bridge of financing while

funds from other sources were being mobilized. It provided advantageous conditions for contingent financing and

was an incentive for implementing critical policy reforms to strengthen long-term climate and disaster resilience and

health emergency preparedness.

  Original Policy Areas/Pillars Supported by the Program (as approved)



  10. The operation was structured around three pillars, with four prior actions, that addressed the identified policy

  gaps (as elaborated in Section II A).



  •   Pillar A: Improving the climate and disaster risk management institutional and regulatory framework. Policy

      reform under this pillar aimed to enhance the GoN’s institutional and technical capacity for disaster risk

      reduction and management through an enabling regulatory environment that specifies operational procedures

      and institutional roles and responsibilities of the NDRRMA.

  •   Pillar B: Integrating climate and disaster resilience in key sectors. Policy reforms under this pillar supported (i)

      integrating climate change adaptation and mitigation in the development plans, policies, programs and budgets

      of the federal, provincial and local governments towards building a climate resilience society, and (ii) enhancing

      the seismic resilience of the buildings.

  •   Pillar C: Enhancing human health crisis preparedness and response. Policy reforms under the third pillar

      supported the government’s efforts to strengthen health systems to better prepare and respond to pandemics

      and epidemics with defined roles for the different tiers of government to manage disease outbreaks.



  B. SIGNIFICANT CHANGES DURING IMPLEMENTATION

  11. There were no changes in the Program design (PDO, pillars, and indicators) during the four-and-a-half-year

  implementation period. The operation’s closing date was extended by 18 months from May 30, 2023, to November

  30, 2024 (see paragraph 52).



   II. ASSESSMENT OF KEY PROGRAM DESIGN AND OUTCOMES

  12. The Nepal Development Policy Financing (DPF) with Cat DDO was the country’s first access to disaster risk

  financing instrument strategically designed to provide access to immediate liquidity during crises while supporting

  critical institutional and regulatory reforms for long-term climate, disaster and health risk management. The design

  of the program was innovative as this was among the first operations in South Asia Region (and globally) to integrate

  disaster and health emergency preparedness in the policy matrix. This was the third instance in South Aisa Region and

  globally, following Maldives and Bhutan Cat DDOs, where the alignment of health and disaster response interventions

  was a central part of the program design from the outset, even before the COVID-19 pandemic, and this feature was

  sustained under the Second Cat DDO.

  13. The DPF with Cat DDO was a single-tranche operation with an initial 3-year drawdown period. The operation

  was approved on March 10, 2020, and was disbursed in full by April 2024.

  A. RELEVANCE OF PRIOR ACTIONS

      Rating: Satisfactory

  15. The relevance of prior actions (PAs) is Satisfactory, with all four PAs demonstrating a clear and direct link to the

  achievement of the PDO. The PDO was clearly articulated, emphasizing the enhancement of the institutional and

  regulatory capacity of the GoN to manage climate and disaster risks and health emergencies. It reflected Nepal’s rapid

  development over the previous two decades, its proactive approach towards adverse natural events and climate

  change, and its commitment to a resilient and sustainable development agenda. The PAs further demonstrate GoN’s

  commitment to policy reform in climate and disaster risk management and health emergency preparedness. These

  reforms were designed to help mitigate both physical and fiscal impacts of such risks, while enhancing Nepal’s

  preparedness and response capacity for large-scale disasters. The PAs built on the national and international priorities

  and initiatives on climate, disaster and health risk management. The policy frameworks such as the Fifteenth Five Year

  Development Plan (2019/20-2023/24), the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2017, the National

  Disaster Risk Reduction Policy of 2018, the Disaster Risk Reduction National Strategic Plan of Action for 2018 to 2030,

  the first Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) of 2016, the Climate Change Policy of 2019, the Climate Change

  Budget Coding practice since 2013, the Nepal Health Sector Strategy for 2015 to 2020, and the experience of disease

  surveillance and Early Warning and Reporting System and the operationalization of Health Emergency Operation

  Center informed the formulation of the PAs. In addition, the selection of prior actions and result indicators was

  informed by the progress in the implementation of the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015-2030 and

  its priority area on strengthening disaster risk governance, investing in disaster reduction for resilience, and enhancing

  disaster preparedness for effective response.

       Pillar A: Improving the climate and disaster risk management institutional and regulatory framework

       Result Indicator 1: National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority established and operational

       Baseline (2020): No | Target (2024): Yes | Result at closing: Yes



       20. The RI target was achieved. NDRRMA was established and operational, with own office space within the

       Government of Nepal’s main administrative complex (Singhadurbar), with 38 approved staff (based on the

       Organization and Management Survey) and additional 21 staff seconded by different organizations at the NDRRMA for

       better coordination and execution of its functions.



       21. The establishment of the NDRRMA marks a significant milestone in Nepal’s commitment to disaster resilience.

       This was the first time an authority was instituted to coordinate and manage disasters, which were previously managed

       by the Ministry of Home Affairs and focused primarily on response and recovery. Disaster risk reduction, preparedness,

       response, reconstruction and recovery, were all streamlined and integrated into the disaster risk management

       functions of the NDRRMA aligning with the DRRM Act of 2017 and the first regulation (PA3). The establishment and

       operationalization of NDRRMA have significantly enhanced coordination aspect by implementing a unified command

       system, strengthened local capacities through targeted capacity building initiatives, and brought greater clarity to

       national response and recovery planning compared to the approach taken prior to NDRRMA’s establishment.



       22. Strategic policymaking was a cornerstone of NDRRMA’s success. In rapid succession following its formation, the

       NDRRMA oversaw the development of 25 key policy frameworks, procedures, strategies, and guidelines, most notably

       the Shock Responsive Social Protection and Relief Distribution Standard. This new standard has streamlined disaster

       relief by leveraging existing social protection systems, enhancing both efficiency and transparency in resource

       distribution at critical times. NDRRMA’s capacity was tested immediately after its establishment with the responsibility

       of assuming the responsibility of the National Reconstruction Authority (NRA) that was leading the Nepal’s largest post-

       disaster housing reconstruction project, the EHRP. NDRRMA also effectively assumed the role of the key focal agency

       for the Cat DDO operation, which enabled the quick mobilization of the Cat DDO resources in response to the floods

       and landslides in 2020 and earthquakes in 2023. Though the World Bank does not monitor the specific use of the

       Cat DDO resources, however, based on the information from NDRRMA, these resources helped replenish the Central

       Disaster Management Fund to support disaster response efforts, including transfers to local and district Disaster

       Management Funds.



       23. NDRRMA’s impact continues to grow as it strengthens its organizational foundation. A recent Organization and

       Management Survey propose expanding the staff to 68 6 , further reinforcing the NDRRMA capacity to fulfill its

       mandate. NDRRMA has institutionalized the Monsoon Preparedness Plan as an annual, nationwide initiative,

       coordinated disaster management efforts across the country, exemplifying its leadership in disaster risk management.

     Drawdown of Cat DDO

     34. The Cat DDO resources were drawn down twice and were mobilized swiftly to respond to the impacts of floods

     and landslides in 2020, and earthquakes in 2023.



     35. First drawdown of the Cat DDO resources. During the 2020 monsoon (June to September), Nepal experienced

     unprecedented rainfall leading to 494 incidents of flashfloods and landslides, causing over 365 deaths, 100 missing

     persons, and 325 injuries. The preliminary losses and damages were estimated at US$100 million. In response, the

     government declared disaster areas9 in Sindhupalchowk10 and Baglung11 districts for period between September 22

     to October 16, 2020, and requested a US$25 million Cat DDO withdrawal on September 27, 2020. The World Bank

     confirmed that the drawdown condition was met and communicated the acceptance of evidence furnished on

     September 30, 2020. The Ministry of Finance (MoF) submitted a withdrawal application for the approved amount on

     October 2, 2020, and it was processed and disbursed on the same day.



     36. Following the first drawdown, the country was impacted by various disasters (flash floods and landslides), and

     even a state of disaster emergency 12 was declared for the monsoon-related disasters in Manang and

     Sindhupalchowk district on July 26, 2021. However, a disbursement request was not presented to the Bank, as the

     government preferred to use its own domestic resources to respond to the disaster.

     C. OVERALL OUTCOME RATING AND JUSTIFICATION

         Rating: Satisfactory



     39. The overall outcome of this operation is rated Satisfactory. The PAs had a strong and direct link to the Cat DDO’s

     objectives and were aligned with the Government’s priorities. The targets of the RIs were achieved earlier than the

     target year, with two RIs exceeding their targets. Technical assistance provided by the WB (see Table 2) supported the

     implementation of the policy reforms and subsequently the achievement of the targets of the RIs. The operation

     enabled the implementation of critical policy reforms to enhance the technical and institutional capacity of the Nepal

     government to manage climate and disaster risks, including disease outbreaks. Moreover, the Cat DDO provided an

     efficient and immediate liquidity during the disaster emergency and thus was crucial for Nepal’s capacity to respond

     to disaster emergencies. The Cat DDO provided advantageous conditions for contingent financing and was an

     incentive that pushed forward critical policy reforms for long-term climate and disaster risk management, including

     disease outbreaks, as well as for a subsequent Cat DDO operation.



     40. The operation provided an avenue to engage in a dialogue with the Government of Nepal to roll out the Bank’s

     Crisis Preparedness and Response Toolkit (CPRT). The continuous policy dialogue with the Government led to

     adoption of the Rapid Response Option (RRO). Nepal was a first country in South Asia to do so.

     D. OTHER UNINTENDED OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS



     45. The operation fostered continued dialogue with key stakeholders regarding the improvement and compliance

     of building codes, which resulted in technical assistance being provided to the DUDBC by the development partners.

     In 2021, JICA initiated a project—Promotion of Nepal National Building Code Compliance for Safer Building

     Construction—for the effective roll out of the NBC105:2020 at the local level. Key activities included expanding and

     strengthening the electronic Building Permit System, as well as building the capacity of local engineers in

     implementing the code and the working procedures. Guidelines were also developed to implement the NBC 105 in

     pilot municipalities in Kathmandu valley.



     46. The Cat DDO contributed to strengthening technical capacities to implement NBCs, including NBC 105. The

     Nepal Engineering Association, together with DUDBC, and the engineering associations rolled out training programs

     in all the provinces for local government officials and engineers. Moreover, metropolitan cities of Kathmandu and

     Lalitpur integrated NBC 105 provisions in the Building Permit System.

     VI. LESSONS AND NEXT PHASE



     A.    LESSONS LEARNED



     56. Nepal’s Cat DDO proved its dual value as an immediate liquidity backstop and a reform anchor in a high disaster

     risk environment. It enabled a rapid drawdown of US$25million after severe monsoon-triggered catastrophic floods

     and landslides in 2020, and subsequent drawdown of US$25million after the Jajarkot earthquake in 2023. This ensured

     immediate liquidity to address the post-disaster needs while simultaneously locking in policy and institutional reforms

     through i) operationalizing the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority, ii) mainstreaming climate

     action through provincial coordination committees, iii) mandating seismic-resilient construction via the updated Nepal

     National Building Code (NBC 105) with full compliance for government buildings and iv) strengthened health

     emergency preparedness that was tested during COVID-19 and through targeted simulations at points of entry. The

     lessons learned from this operation informed the subsequent Cat DDO development.



     57. Strong government ownership and alignment with national priorities are critical for ensuring the sustainability

     of reforms. The reforms targeted in this operation became embedded in the country’s disaster risk management and

     health agendas, which secured high level political buy-in and facilitated smoother implementation despite capacity and

     resource constraints. For example, implementation of NBC 105 was constrained by a lack of resources and capacity;

     however, the DUDBC was able to enforce seismic provisions in newly built public buildings, indicating the broader

     ownership of the reforms and their importance for long-term disaster resilience.



     58. Integrating the Cat DDO into a broader DRM program and complementing it with targeted technical assistance

     can significantly enhance its effectiveness. The operation benefitted greatly from WB’s prior engagement in DRM

     specifically the EHRP and Nepal Earthquake Multi-donor Trust Fund supported activities. The success of this Cat DDO

B. NEXT PHASE



61. Recognizing the country’s significant fiscal exposure to disasters, the GoN requested a second Cat DDO

operation along with an eighteen-month extension of the ongoing Cat DDO, to ensure uninterrupted access to

contingent financing until the new operation became effective. The Bank worked collaboratively with the

Government to develop the new operation, while facilitating the final disbursement of contingent financing to

respond to the disaster emergency caused by the Jajarkot Earthquake in November 2023. The subsequent new Cat

DDO built upon the achievements of this operation and continued to fill the policy gaps.
