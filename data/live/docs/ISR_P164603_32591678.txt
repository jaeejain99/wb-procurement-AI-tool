 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The Ghana Productive Safety Nets (GPSNP) became effective in June 2019, and project implementation on the ground began shortly thereafter. In

  March 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic caused a temporary slowdown in project implementation and procurement. Given the critical role of safety

  nets in providing relief to affected populations, working with the World Bank and other development partners, the government began to adjust

  program design and implementation to offer COVID relief, to integrate COVID-safe procedures for delivery, and to account for the infection

  containment measures put in place by the government.

  Key areas of progress include the following:

       1. The Labor-Intensive Public Works (LIPW) reduced work hours and administered a shift system to promote social distancing, while

            maintaining full pay for beneficiaries. Despite the initial delays, the following results were achieved: (i) 32,674 beneficiaries were enrolled

            by July 2020, against a target of 30,000 beneficiaries; (ii) 62 percent of beneficiaries are female against the target of 60 percent; (iii) 35

            percent of works at small earth dams and feeder road rehabilitation subprojects were completed; and (iv) 7.9 million cashew and other

            assorted seedlings for LIPW community plantations were under production.

        2. The LEAP cash transfers program has (i) provided bi-monthly payments through the first 10 months of 2020 to 333,908 households

            (approximately 1.4 million individuals), with 55 percent of beneficiaries being female; (ii) developed emergency payments, communication,

            and monitoring guidelines to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic; and (iii) provided transportation funds for beneficiaries to travel to banks

            to receive their transfers given that community-based cash-outs and gatherings were banned, and (iv) purchased health and sanitary

            items such as sanitizers, hand washing tools and masks for program beneficiaries and personnel.

        3. The Ghana National Household Registry (GNHR) successfully (i) completed data collection of 376,206 households (out of an expected

            400,000 households) in the Northern, Savannah, and North East regions in October 2020 and (ii) developed and utilized a robust

            management information system for managing field data collection activities. Additionally, during the partial lockdown period in April 2020,

            the GNHR adapted its systems to collect and disseminate emergency data on 78,741 homeless individuals in the Greater Accra Region,

            which is being utilized by government and development partners to support poor and vulnerable people.

        4. The Call Center of the Single Window Citizens Engagement Service (SWCES) recorded a total of 1,978 cases between January and

            September 2020, of which 1,750 cases (88 percent) were resolved. The majority of cases (1,315) were received during the partial

            lockdown of the Greater Accra and Kumasi areas in April 2020.

  Moving forward, there is a need to accelerate critical procurement activities that were delayed as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic globally, in

  support of stronger project implementation performance and progress on the ground. The project has embarked on a strategy for greater

  mainstreaming of project implementation to promote sustainability of the project's activities within government. This includes increased inclusion and

  training of civil service staff, assessing capacity at the regional and community levels for implementation, and establishing data storage systems and

  protocols.
