 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Both project components continue to achieve key milestones, bringing each entity closer to achieving project objectives. The project continues to

  exceed expectations on most of the project’s key results indicators, particularly on the number of households reached with climate friendly products,

  while lagging but catching up on the third PDO indicator related to increased revenuers of firms trained.



  Two aspects of the project remain the focus of project implementation support: (i) the implementation of a strong sustainability strategy for the

  GCIC; and (ii) the progress on governance, investment, and fundraising by the GCVF to ensure the fund’s viability beyond the Bank grant.

  Project Closing Date Extension:

  With the unexpected onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, elements of the current year project work plans have been impacted, resulting in some

  delays to implementation. For this reason, the Bank is planning an extension of the project closing date from June 30, 2020 to September 30, 2020.

  The extension will enable both GCIC and GCVF to complete ongoing activities, including those that have been delayed as a result of the COVID-19

  pandemic, and continue fundraising, adding to the likelihood of sustainability of the project's impacts after the Bank financing ends.



  GCIC (Component 1) Implementation Progress:

  The GCIC has incubated 101 businesses since project inception and is recognized as an important driver of green growth in Ghana. To date, the

  GCIC has incubated five cohorts of entrepreneurs, with 30 entrepreneurs in its fifth cohort, almost doubling the number in prior cohorts. An

  additional 69 start-ups and small and medium enterprises (SMEs) have been supported by the GCIC through bootcamps and other short course

  events since 2019 including a global Climate Launchpad competition, a water innovation lab, and a “breaking barriers” event as part of the GCIC’s

  Incubating Innovation annual symposium. Programming for GCIC’s current cohort is being delivered virtually, adhering to the government’s social

  distancing precautions. To maximize current resources, and in anticipation of securing future funding, the GCIC has decided to defer welcoming its

  sixth cohort until funding is secured. The GCIC had instead planned to hold several shorter “bootcamp” type trainings for entrepreneurs. One

  planned bootcamp in March had to be cancelled due to the pandemic onset. Beyond its core incubation business, an important event for the GCIC

  is its annual Incubating Innovation Symposium which attracts over 200 green economy stakeholders in Ghana including policy makers, investors,

  technology providers, entrepreneurship organizations, and potential funders. This year’s symposium was a series of events held from early March.

  Unfortunately, the culminating event, originally scheduled for March 19, was postponed due to COVID-19. With the end of the lockdown and an

  extension of the grant closing to September 30, the GCIC will seek to reschedule these events as possible. A "Climate Focus" documentary series

  featuring GCIC entrepreneurs will be featured on Ghanaian television in May and June 2020.



  Disbursements: The GCIC component is at 92% disbursement and is on track to disburse the full $8.5m RE grant by project closing. GCIC has also

  established good leads on new funding with bilateral donors, government, foundations, and corporates and has hired an additional climate finance

  consulting firm to help accelerate the building of its revenue streams and assist with short-term bridge funding.



  GCVF (Component 2) Implementation Progress:

  The GCVF grant was signed in October 2018 and the investment fund was launched in June 2019. It operates in the market as Wangara Green

  Ventures. Wangara has established strong governance structures as required in the grant agreement including a grant Steering Committee, the

  Wangara Green Ventures board of directors, and the Wangara Investment Committee. Wangara is fully staffed with an investment team and back

  office. Following the restructuring, the GCVF is now implementing at the expected pace, currently focused on further building up its investment

  pipeline and raising additional public and private investment for its capital pool. The GCVF has built an investment pipeline of over 90 leads,

  conducted due diligence on eight businesses, and has had three investments approved by its investment committee. It is also providing direct

  technical assistance to six potential investee companies and has referred three prospects to the GCIC for investment readiness training. The impact

  of the COVID-19 pandemic and the related restrictions set in place interrupted some elements of the GCVF work plan, including due diligence on

  additional investment opportunities and the raising of additional investment capital. These activities have both been adjusted to the extent possible

  to virtual means, but there are still delayed timelines and slowed progress.



  Disbursements: The GCVF component is at 66% disbursement of the $3.2m RE grant.



  Withdrawal Condition Modification: Wangara Green Ventures, the fund manager for the GCVF, has achieved the governance and investment

  targets in the GCVF grant agreement required to withdrawal the full grant funds. However, it has not yet achieved the matching fundraising target

  (USD 2m of matching capital) in the grant agreement. Given the impacts on fundraising from COVID-19 and the progress made on all other aspects

  of GCVF implementation, the Bank is planning an adjustment to the withdrawal condition for a final USD 1m disbursement to allow USD 500K

  capital to be disbursed on the investment targets and USD 500K capital to be disbursed on the basis of its fundraising targets and allowing Letters

  of Commitment as an alternative to Shareholders Agreements to signify investor commitment. This would recognize the progress on investment

  targets while leaving in place incentives to meet fundraising targets



  Results

  Both project components are moving closer to achieving results targets. As of the end of Q1 2020, two of three PDO level indicators are exceeding

  targets, namely: (i) households accessing climate friendly solutions, which has reached over 340,000 Ghanaian households, far surpassing the

  initial target of 20,000; and (ii) the number of participants in workshops, trainings, and other events, which has increased to 1,267, exceeding the

  initial target of 1,100. The third PDO level indicator on increased revenues of targeted businesses is below target, with USD 2,590,980 achieve

  against the end target of USD 4,500,000. While this indicator is below target, significant improvements have been made since the prior ISR.

  Importantly, component 2 has contributed results to the revenues indicator for the first time. Both components contribute to the results, however

  component 2 targets businesses with much higher revenues than component 1 and is now expected to make higher contributions to this indicator

  than component 1 through project closing.



  Intermediate indicators are also being achieved in most cases. The number of businesses supported has reached 176 which is almost double the

  target of 90 businesses. Direct jobs created is now 153 net (i.e. including gains and any losses to those same jobs), above the target of 96 for the

  project. Indirect jobs, while not captured systematically, are also being created. For instance, over 600 women in the Northern and Upper Regions

  An intermediate indicator that has not been achieved is the top line measure of new laws / regulations / amendments / codes / Government policies

  drafted or contributed to the drafting, which is defined as new laws passed as a direct outcome of the GCIC’s public-private dialogue (PPD)

  sessions. Zero against the target of four for the project duration has been achieved. However, other measures captured by the GCIC demonstrate

  that the project as supported the policy making process considerably. The GCIC has recorded 13 Public Private Dialogue sessions with policy

  makers in attendance. The GCIC has also had 205 media appearances, 44 knowledge products developed, and 33 knowledge-sharing events

  hosted – all increasing the GCIC’s reach and relevance to policy. The GCIC has submitted a direct contribution to the Ministry of Science,

  Technology and Innovation’s science, technology and innovation (STI) draft government policy. The mission was informed that the policy, yet to be

  adopted, is being amended to incorporate GCIC’s submission.



  The project continues to reach women-owned businesses, including through the GCIC’s women entrepreneurs’ transformation program

  (https://www.ghanacic.org/about/women-entrepreneurship-transformation-program-wetp/). 32% of businesses supported by the project are women-

  owned and 41% of direct jobs created employ women, exceeding the 20% target for both indicators.



  Climate emission reduction outcomes are measured by the implementing partners (though emissions are not part of the project indicators as

  households reached with climate solutions was selected as the project’s climate measure). There have been 5,497 MT of carbon dioxide emissions

  avoided cumulatively from the project. This represents a significant increase in the emissions avoided compared to the previous quarter – Q4 2019

  – due largely to an increase in solar installations.



  Finally, an important result targeted is sustainability of both the GCIC and GCVF beyond project close. If this can be achieved, continued impact of

  the project would be expected beyond the project end.
