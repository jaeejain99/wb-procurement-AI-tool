 4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
The project is making strong overall progress towards its development objectives of improving access to human capital
services for vulnerable host communities and displaced Rohingya populations, with cumulative disbursement reaching
over US$103.33 million — 28% of the net IDA commitment. 84% of the project’s total value is committed in the
Systematic Tracking of Exchanges in Procurement (STEP); all 13 UN contracts are signed.

Component 1 has already achieved approximately 64% of its four-year targets within the second year of implementation,
with around 631,897 DRP and host community households reached through livelihood and workfare services, 22,000
host community individuals benefiting from short-term employment, and the Employment Generation Program for the
Poorest Plus (EGPP+) generating 12,519,829 person-days of work — reaching 55.83% of the four-year goal. On
education, the MoPME-UNICEF agreement has been formally signed to provide basic education support to
approximately 250,000 children in the camps, while the host community School Feeding Program is set to commence in
1,095 schools across Cox's Bazar and Bandarban from mid-April 2026. Child protection interventions under Component
3, implemented with UNICEF, have reached over 90,000 children, adolescents, parents, and caregivers through the
National Child Helpline, awareness campaigns, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), life skills training,
and case management services, complemented by a landmark academic partnership with Dhaka University for a
Graduate Diploma in Child Protection. On health and nutrition, Health facilities have performed 87,391 normal deliveries
in host communities. Additionally, 240,012 adolescent girls aged 10–19 years received iron and folate supplementation
across both host and displaced/refugee populations. GBV response services have further recorded 345,411 cumulative
visits across 55 Women-Friendly Spaces and 5 One-Stop Crisis Centers.

A joint ERD-World Bank workshop to take stock of results and support problem solving was held in October 2025. In
March 2026, ERD has initiated publication of a monthly newsletter to document results, challenges and overall
implementation performance of this Inclusive Services and Opportunities for Host Communities and Displaced Rohingya
Population (ISO) project along with other active response projects working on the same target groups.
