 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The US$35 million Credit to finance the Public Sector Reform for Results Project (PSRRP) was approved by the Board of Executive Directors of the

  World Bank on October 23, 2018. The Financing Agreement was signed on November 12, 2018, and the project was declared effective on May 8,

  2019.

  The PSRRP supports Ghana’s National Public Sector Reform Strategy, 2018-2023. It helps 16 agencies improve efficiency and accountability in

  delivery of key public services to citizens and firms, such as issuance and renewal of passports, vehicle registration and environmental permits, and

  registration of births and deaths. To incentivize these sixteen entities to improve, disbursement-linked indicators (DLIs) will reward entities which

  achieve agreed results and improve public accountability. Implementation support will be provided by the World Bank to strengthen the entities’

  capacity to achieve the agreed results.

  The project emphasizes public accountability and reporting, including through a Project Steering Committee (PStC) which will, among other things:

  (i) review PSRRP implementation progress and achievement of results; (ii) hold semi-annual public meetings where the sixteen entities will each

  submit a report on their progress and results, and their implementation plans for the next six months; and (iii) publish each such written report, along

  with a video recording of the meeting, on the website of the Office of Senior Minister (OSM) and require an Independent Verification Agency (IVA) to

  verify the achievement of results stated in each such report. The PtSC's composition (comprising at least three civil society/non-government

  organizations or CSOs), resources and terms of reference are to be acceptable to the World Bank. The selection of three CSOs to the PStC was

  delayed and is still under way as of August 25, 2020: as a result, no public PStC meetings have yet been scheduled by the OSM. Given the

  COVID19 situation, the public meetings are intended to be held in safe online format, with wide advance public notice - hopefully enabling large

  media and public participation in the interest of transparency and public accountability.

  The OSM under the Office of the President (OP) is responsible for implementing and managing the project. The project has disbursed $4.35 million

  as of August 31, 2020.

  Although some implementation progress has been made, there have been severe implementation delays due to management and other

  weaknesses in the project management unit (PMU). The disclosed project results show that not much has yet been achieved by way of results -

  mainly due to numerous project activities being delayed due to PMU management and capacity challenges. Some delays have also been due to the

  COVID19 pandemic and its effect on working of government entities.

  A World Bank Implementation Support-cum-Supervision Mission was conducted virtually during June-July 2020. As a consequence of the Mission,

  an Action Plan to improve PMU performance was drawn up and is under implementation. As part of this Plan, and to professionalize and strengthen

  PMU functioning, the OSM has selected (1) a Project Manager and (2) a Procurement Specialist. It is expected that their joining, along with the

  recruitment of other experts agreed with the World Bank, will help bring the project back on track, accelerate project activities across entities, and

  begin delivering results. A key focus in the short term will be to implement activities that can supplement the government's COVID19 response and

  enable service delivery entities to provide - and receive payment for - selected services in a safe and contactless manner.

 Overall Comments PDO



  About half the quantitative and nominal indicators have achieved zero results yet while progress on qualitative/text indicators is not substantial.

  This is due to the significant delays in starting project activities by the PMU, even in cases where the relevant entity had submitted the terms of

  reference or the request for goods procurement. As reported in other sections, PMU management and capacity weaknesses have had

  unfortunate consequences on project activities, disbursements and results.



  Considering the importance of results to the success of the project, the team will closely monitor the Action Plan items to improve PMU

  performance and these will be followed up on robustly. The team will consider a downgrade of both PDO and IP ratings if substantial progress

  is seen.



  Moreover, as the team begins to think through project restructuring with counterparts, we will examine some of the results indicators with a

  view to determining their continued relevance and feasibility of achievement within the context of the implementation pace of the project.
