I. PROJECT CONTEXT AND DEVELOPMENT OBJECTIVES


  A. CONTEXT AT APPRAISAL


  Country Context

  1.       At the time of appraisal, Bangladesh, one of the world’s most populous countries with an estimated
  population of 165 million people, in a geographical area of about 144,415 sq.-km had a per capita income of
  US$1,480 in 2017, well above the lower middle income country category threshold which it crossed in fiscal year
  (FY) 14. During the same period, economic conditions improved in the country with headline inflation declining to 5.4
  percent in FY17 from 5.9 percent in FY16, while the fiscal deficit was contained at around 3 percent of Gross Domestic
  Product (GDP) in FY17. The FY18 budget targets 5 percent deficit with 26.2 percent growth in expenditures. The
  current account balance turned into a deficit equivalent to 0.6 percent of GDP in FY17. The GDP grew well above the
  average for developing countries in recent years, averaging 6.5 percent since 2010, with an officially reported growth
  of 7.24 percent in FY17, driven by manufacturing and services.

  2.      Progress on reducing extreme poverty and boosting shared prosperity through human development and
  employment generation had continued to improve. The poverty incidence based on the international $1.90 per
  capita per day poverty line (measured based on the 2011 Purchasing Power Parity exchange rate) declining from 44.2
  percent in 1991 to a 13.8 percent in 2016 (latest available poverty data). Bangladesh’s performance against the
  Millennium Development Goals was impressive against the South Asia Region average. Such progress
  notwithstanding, the pace of poverty reduction and the rate of job creation had slowed since 2010. Bangladesh
  needed more effort in improving its growth rate to meet its target of moving up the middle-income rankings by 2021
  and eliminating poverty by 2030. For accelerating private sector-led growth with improved investment climate, the
  key challenges were the need for increased infrastructure and power, with much improved quality in public resources
  spending, better regulations, and enhanced skills of country’s vast and rapidly increasing labor force.

   Sectoral and Institutional Context

  3.       The Government’s Seventh Five Year Plan (FY16–FY20, the 7th Five Year Plan) aims to develop strategies,
  policies, and institutions to accelerate inclusive growth, reduce poverty, empower citizens, and promote
  sustainable development. It adopted the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), to which the
  Government of Bangladesh (GOB) has shown strong commitment. The GOB has expressed commitment to monitor
  progress towards the achievement of goals and acknowledges the importance of and is committed to the timely
  availability of reliable statistics for evidence-based planning and policy making, as reflected in its National Strategy
  for the Development of Statistics (NSDS).

  4.       The GOB has taken significant steps to improve the availability, quality, and reliability of statistics. In 2007,
  the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS), under the Ministry of Planning, responsible for providing statistical
  information to guide decision making and the development process, embarked upon a three-stage process to improve
  statistics, with support from the World Bank. The first stage, from 2007 to 2009, was limited to improving the coverage
  and quality of statistics in key areas. The second stage, completed in 2011, involved the preparation of the first NSDS,
  which maps out a development path for the National Statistical System (NSS) to have a world-class system by 2021.

        The GOB approved the NSDS 1 in 2013, together with the Statistics Act 2013, that envisages an “integrated,
        professional, efficient, and effective NSS, under the guidance and leadership of the BBS and to produce official
        statistics that meet the current and evolving needs of national and international users in a transparent and timely
        fashion using international standards”. The Statistics Act 2013 confers on the BBS the function of “preparing and
        preserving correct, accurate, and up-to-date statistics.” Other agencies may also produce statistics “following the
        regulations established by the bureau and upon getting no objection and approval from the bureau.”2 The third stage
        (2013–2023) of the process is putting the NSDS into effect.

        5.     The NSDS Implementation Support Project (the proposed project) aimed to support the GOB in the
        implementation of key elements of the NSDS. The NSDS is a ten-year reform plan (2013–2023) costed at around
        US$600 million with four priority areas:

                I.         Improving the quality, coverage, and use of core statistics required for national planning and
                           economic management and for monitoring progress towards national goals.
                II.        Strengthening the professionalism of the NSS, which involves, among others, increasing the skills and
                           expertise of the workforce, enhancing the leadership capacity of the BBS, and strengthening
                           procedures and internal processes to make the statistical system more open and accountable.
               III.        Building capacity to collect, compile, disseminate, and, especially, use statistics at the local level,
                           providing statistical information to help empower citizens.
               IV.         Promoting and strengthening access to and the use of official statistics at all levels of the society,
                           based on an ‘open-data strategy’.

        6.      The project, consistent with the priorities of the NSDS, aimed to focus on: (a) improving data collection and
        the quality of data in several areas, including gender disaggregation where appropriate and (b) strengthening capacity
        of the BBS for better management and dissemination of improved statistics. The proposed project will build on the
        progress made thus far in some reform areas of the NSDS while complementing investments by the GOB and other
        development partners (DPs).

        Higher Level Objectives to which the Project aimed to Contribute.

        7.       There was a strong rationale for World Bank engagement. The World Bank had a mandate to support
        statistical capacity activities, which was central for monitoring of World Bank Group’s twin goals of eradicating poverty
        and boosting shared prosperity and to measuring progress towards the newly endorsed SDGs. 3 This priority was
        reflected in the IDA15 Results Measurement System that requires all Country Assistance or Partnership Frameworks
        to include a review of national statistical systems and an identification of what is needed to strengthen capacity.
        Further, ‘better governance in 30 countries through improved statistical capacity’ was one of the action items in the
        IDA18 commitments.

        8.      The project was also in line with the Country Partnership Framework (CPF) for Bangladesh FY16–FY20 which
        highlighted World Bank’s support for the NSDS. In particularly, support for the NSDS responded directly to the CPF’s

      Theory of Change (Results Chain)

      9.      The project’s results chain4 built on the need to strengthen the NSS by building the capacity of BBS to
      produce quality core statistics and make them accessible in a timely manner to policy makers and the public. The
      project activities sought to build BBS capacity by acting on three levels (Figure 1). First, there was need for an enabling
      environment where the statistical system has the necessary legal and institutional support to conduct its business.
      This entailed updating statistical legislation, policies, and institutional arrangements. Second, it was key to improve
      BBS’s organization and the production of statistics by strengthening management systems, improving human and ICT
      resources, and the statistical infrastructure. Third, the project sought to focus on strengthening user-producer
      dialogue so that statistics are used and users’ feedback into the production cycle.

      10.      Although the NSDS ISP project encountered delays at the start, it has made considerable contributions.
      Among other supported activities, BBS was able to conduct functional review of BBS to strengthen management
      systems, implement several trainings following the development of a training plan and policy, conducted and released
      data as well as relevant reports from the 2022 Population and Housing Census (PHC), the 2022 Household Income
      and Expenditure Survey, and the 2022 quarterly LFS survey. The PHC, HIES, and LFS were the first major census and
      surveys to utilize the Computer Assisted Personal Interview (CAPI) method for data collection. The project further
      supported preparation of the ICT Plan and Roadmap to improve human and ICT resources and the statistical
      infrastructure, and the improvement in data collection, production, and dissemination through the implementation
      of other household surveys, improvement of national accounts and price statistics, health statistics, and agriculture
      statistics. Project established the National Advisory Council on Statistics (NACS) to provide guidance and set national
      priorities on statistics and signed five MoU’s with national and international data producers, including Statistics Korea
      (KOSTAT) to enhance coordination.

      11.      These initiatives contributed to the achievement of the PDO by improving core survey methodologies in
      filling data gaps. In addition, the project strengthened user-producer dialogue and supported the improvement of
      data access and dissemination by financing the (a) BBS’ first ever user satisfaction survey, (b) design of the new BBS
      website with an advance release calendar and protocols for the timely release of survey statistics and (c) formulation
      and adoption of Data Dissemination Policy and Microdata Anonymization Manual access policies, an aspect that is
      key to enabling evidence-based policy making and social accountability. The project further provided other technical
      support, including survey documentation, microdata anonymization, data visualization, metadata documentation,
      preparation of codes and syntaxes, and produced reports and documentation on administrative data, report on HR
      recruitment, training Policy, training Plan, training inventory, dissemination policy, and protocol for advance release
      calendar, among others.

     Components

     16.     Component A: Improving the Coordination and Management of Statistical Activities. This component
     focused on improving coordination among other data producers and strengthening statistical management systems
     of BBS. To improve coordination, existing Memorandum of Understandings (MOUs) between the BBS and other
     government agencies on data sharing and access were reviewed and a Report on Memorandum of Understanding
     (MOU) was produced, suggesting areas where improvements are needed to enhance coordination.

     17.      Component B: Developing Human Resources and ICT Infrastructure to Produce and Manage Date. This
     component aimed at strengthening the role of the Statistical Staff Training Institute of the BBS and developing a
     training policy to deliver systematic training for BBS staff.

     18.      Component C: Improving the Coverage and Quality of Core Statistics Required for Policy Making. This
     component supported the improvement of the design, implementation, and information generated by high priority;
     core statistical operations identified in the NSDS. The core statistical operations included national accounts, price
     statistics, social statistics, and agricultural statistics.

     19.     Component D: Promoting and Strengthening Access and the Use of Official Statistics. This component
     promoted and strengthened access to official statistics, contributing to priority area 4 of the NSDS by implementing
     a dissemination policy, improving access to statistical products and metadata, and enhancing user-producer dialogue.
     Other activities included staff training on metadata preparation, adhering to international standards, anonymizing
     microdata, and providing wide access to data.

     20.     Component E: Project Management. This component supported the establishment of the Project
     Implementation Unit (PIU) at the BBS, responsible for ensuring the day-to-day coordination, implementation, and
     management in accordance with the Project Operations Manual (POM), including fiduciary aspects, monitoring,
     evaluation, and reporting. The PIU prepared annual work plans and budgets, managed disbursements and
     procurement activities, consolidated progress reports, and monitored and evaluated project activities.

Other Changes

25.      The project was restructured three times during implementation.

26.     The project’s first restructuring was approved by the World Bank on March 22, 2020. The restructuring aimed
to extend the closing date of the project from February 28, 2022, to August 31, 2022.

27.     The project’s second restructuring was approved by the World Bank on September 19, 2021. The
restructuring aimed to extend the closing date of the project from August 31, 2022, to June 30, 2023, and introduce
changes to the Results Framework.

28.    The project’s third restructuring was approved by the World Bank on January 29, 2023. The restructuring
aimed to extend the closing date of the project from June 30, 2023, to February 27, 2024, and introduce changes to
the Results Framework.

Rationale for Changes and Their Implication on the Original Theory of Change

29.      The rationale for the first restructuring: The overall progress of the project was slow, which was attributed
to delays in: (i) hiring consultants for the Project PIU; and (ii) the procurement of the Twinning Partner (TP). The BBS
faced considerable challenges in finding the right candidates for the positions of the Procurement Specialist and
Financial Management Specialist in the PIU, with the latter being a disbursement condition in the Project. There were
also delays in the hiring of consultants to carry out key activities identified in the annual work plan. This was mainly
due to the long processes BBS needed to follow for hiring international consultants, including getting approval from
the Economic Relations Division (ERD) and the Ministry of Planning. The six-month extension of the closing date was
supposed to provide time for the TP to perform all the activities in the contract and, thereby, help BBS achieve timely
results towards the Project Development Objective, which remained the same.

30.    The rationale for the second restructuring: As part of the findings of the Mid-Term Review (MTR) as well as
the GoB request, two key changes were addressed under the restructuring:

         (i)     Extension of project closing date - The project experienced initial delays in some of the foundational
                 activities including hiring of project consultants for the PIU and hiring of the Twinning Partner (TP).
                 Although implementation gained momentum once the TP was onboarded, COVID-19 pandemic
                 slowed down the pace of progress considerably. Three national lockdowns imposed by the GOB from
                 March-May 2020, from April-May 2021, and June-July 2021 put a hold to the implementation.
                 Movement of key project staff and consultants, both domestic and international, were severely

all levels of skill categories of staff of BBS. These efforts are expected to benefit both the individual staff member as well
as BBS as a whole. A total of 6,455 staff of BBS had been trained under this project in various fields of statistics and the
capacity building of statistical activities.

    C. EFFICIENCY


    Assessment of Efficiency and Rating

    Rating: Substantial

    Economic Analysis

    53.     Official statistics, data production, and analysis are assumed to be public goods, and therefore the project
    does not lend itself to conventional economic cost-benefit analysis. As public goods, official statistics in Bangladesh
    have historically been financed from Government revenue and through contributions from development partners but
    with no expected direct financial return.

    54.      The benefits of the project come from the strengthened capacity of the country’s NSS. The NSDS-ISP
    improved staff capacity to conduct large-scale statistical operations through on-the-job and focused training on
    statistical methodologies and analysis. BBS through the project now has an improved and upgraded office facility, IT
    equipment, and software. Field data collection capability was improved and strengthened through refresher trainings
    for enumerators and building a roster of trained enumerators for future surveys and censuses. BBS through NSDS-ISP
    technical support to BBS in estimating poverty and living conditions of the population using the 2022 HIES, and the
    data were used in the release of the HIES survey report by BBS and are being used by the World Bank to conduct the
    Poverty and Equity Assessment. The Government also benefited from all the data produced and supported through
    the project including the 2022 HIES, 2022 Census, and Quarterly Labor Force Survey, and can utilize it for planning
    and make evidence-based decisions. Researchers and students are also benefiting, using the data to conduct research.

    55.    Moreover, expenses devoted to the production of statistics generate a good return on investment.
    Research in lower middle-income contexts has demonstrated that the availability of quality, transparent, and timely
    household and census data are crucial for social program targeting for poverty reduction and shared prosperity.

    Aspects in Design and Implementation

    56.     The PDO is relevant, realistic, and clear, and encompasses holistic approach to building capacity of BBS while
    ensuring that essential data collection, processing, and dissemination. The technical design of the project is based on
    the needs outlined in the NSDS 2013–2023 and confirmed during consultations with the key stakeholders. The project
    is built upon lessons learned from similar statistical capacity-building activities in the region. It combined
    methodological improvements through technical assistance (TA) and training, equipment, and ICT upgrading. The
    design of the project was in line with international best practices on poverty and living standards survey as well as on
    prices and national account data improvement. In addition, the project addressed the long-term needs of the BBS by
    funding staff development, supporting its transition to an autonomous entity, and improving its infrastructure.

        The World Bank

   57.     The project helped the use the CAPI. The NSDS-ISP supported survey pilots and main survey such as the HIES
   to be carried out by using a CAPI-type collection based on the use of tablets and computers. This helped avoid the use
   of paper questionnaires and reduce long delays for data entry after field work and data processing. With the adoption
   of technology and appropriate training to BBS staff the period for data analysis and release of results were shortened.
   As a result, survey results were disseminated within 1 to 3 months after data collection.

   58.    The project’s key activities were delayed during implementation. NSDS-ISP, which was initially planned to
   be conducted in 2020, was delayed and was successful in completing all project activities in achievement of the four

     64.     NSDS-ISP supported the translation of the, the Statistics Act, 2013 into English, made public through the GoB

     66.      The project did not mobilize private sector financing. It supported activities for the generation and provision of
     statistical products that are public in nature. These offer limited opportunities for revenue generation because public
     funds and official development assistance finance them.

     Poverty Reduction and Shared Prosperity

     67.      The project financed the Household Income and Expenditure Survey for 2022. The poverty lines calculated from
     the data from the BBS HIES survey plays a key role in identifying vulnerable populations and understanding the root
     causes of poverty in Bangladesh. It provides the Government of Bangladesh with the right and timely information to
     design and implement targeted interventions for those in need. The analysis from the HIES is an important resource in
     informing the formulation of the government’s national plans, vision, and strategies, namely the ongoing Five-Year Plans
     (FYP), as well as the Poverty GP’s Poverty and Equity Assessment reports.

     68.     Data from the HIES survey serve as a tool to track progress towards poverty reduction goals and measure the
     effectiveness of interventions. They help understand disparities and inequalities between diverse demographic groups,
     enabling the formulation of policies that promote inclusive economic growth, ensuring that benefits reach marginalized
     communities, and contributing to shared prosperity.

     69.     In addition, the data from HIES are crucial to produce poverty maps at the zila and upazila levels of Bangladesh.
     These maps, based on small area estimation of poverty, are an important tool for policymakers, who rely on the data to
     allocate transfers and inform policy design. Poverty maps are also an important tool for researchers, who use them to
     investigate the relationship between distribution within a country and growth or other economic, environmental, or
     social outcomes.

III. KEY FACTORS AFFECTED IMPLEMENTATION AND OUTCOME


     A. KEY FACTORS DURING PREPARATION

72.    During preparation, the project assessed the sectoral and institutional context and gaps in data production and
dissemination and proposed a way forward. Although BBS had experience in data production, there were rooms for
improvement in quality, coverage and use of core statistics required for national planning and economic management and
for monitoring progress towards national goals.

73.     Strengthening the professionalism of the NSS was identified as a crucial focus area. This involved, among others,
increasing the skills and expertise of the workforce, enhancing the leadership capacity of the BBS, extensive trainings at
the national and local level to build staff capacity.

74.     The project design made it possible to strengthen procedures and internal process to make the statistical system
more open and accountable. Through the TA support the project was able to enhance BBS’s capacity to collect, compile
and disseminate official statistics, thus promoting and strengthening access to data at all levels of the society, based on
an ‘open-data strategy’.

     B. KEY FACTORS DURING IMPLEMENTATION

Factors subject to Control of Government and Implementing Entities

75.     The Government’s commitment to the implementation of the project was strong despite the difficulties at the
start of the project linked to the weak capacity of BBS to ensure the financial management of the project. The
government was able to introduce flexibility to implement the project during the COVID-19 pandemic and revise the TAPP

                                                                                                                       Page 17
         The World Bank
         National Strategy for Development of Statistics Implementation Support (P157987)                    ICR DOCUMENT


when necessary, after each approval of the project restructuring by the Bank.

  Factors Subject to Control by the World Bank

76.     The World Bank task team provided continuous support to the client during project preparation and
implementation. The working team had extensive knowledge of NSDS, BBS as an institution and its operations, producing
and disseminating statistics. Therefore, the project was based on the local situation and context, and it contributed to its
successful design and implementation.

77.      Project benefitted from a smooth transition between the project task team leaders and consultants through
the project’s duration. The task team leadership and versatility were successfully transferred to the PIU core team working
on the project from the design phase and remained until the end of the project, allowing retention of the project’s
institutional memory.

Factors beyond the Control of Government and Implementing Entities

78.      Project implementation faced challenges during the Covid-19 outbreak and related lockdown and travel
restrictions which hampered implementation of key project activities at the height of the pandemic. The government
also introduce restriction for foreign travels of government officials due to low foreign currency reserve in the country.
The PIU, Twinning Partner (TP) and the task team quickly adapted to a virtual modality which was successful in completing
project deliverables on time.

     A. QUALITY OF MONITORING AND EVALUATION (M&E)


 M&E Design

 79.    The project’s M&E framework focuses on accountability for results. To track the PDO and outcomes, the project
 implementation team relied on the project M&E systems and the Results Framework. The Results Framework, as well as
 the PDO and Intermediate results indicators were very clear. Tracking progress towards the intended outputs and
 outcomes was supported by the M&E activities. NSDS PIU played an important role for the M&E function.

 M&E Implementation

 80.     As part of the M&E system, the Bank’s task team, together with the PIU and TP, conducted Implementation
 Status and Results (ISR) Missions every six-months to evaluate the overall performance and progress against PDO and
 IP. During these missions the task team followed up on agreed actions with the client based on the ISR technical notes.
 The various mission reports highlighted key implementation challenges and provided update on all issues,
 recommendations, and progress on actions agreed upon during previous implementation support mission through Aide
 Memoires/technical notes.

 M&E Utilization

 implementation.

Environmental Compliance

at project closure.


Social

84.     Project activities had no negative social impact at the time of appraisal. The project was classified as Category C
and did not trigger any social safeguard policy.

Fiduciary Compliance

85.     The projects procurement performance was generally rated Satisfactory. Priority was given to competitive
bidding process in awarded contracts. The project made effective use of the World Bank’s Systematic Tracking of
Exchanges in Procurement (STEP) tool. Recommendations of the supervision missions were followed up and implemented
in project progress. The projects registered all the documentation of the contracts, deliverables, invoices, and other
payment elements regularly in STEP. No critical were noted in implementing activities approved in the Procurement Plan
during the implementation phase.

the start of the project. The execution rate of the Annual Work Plan and the project disbursement rate were generally
satisfactory. Throughout the implementation period, the project was compliant in submission of acceptable interim
financial report (IFR) and audited financial statements with unqualified opinion. NSDS-ISP regularly ensured the full
implementation of the control recommendations arising from the external audit reports. At the project’s completion, the
disbursement rate was 92.60%.

      C. BANK PERFORMANCE


Quality at Entry

Rating: Satisfactory

87.      The project reflected keen interest and commitment from BBS and the SID to improve the quality, coverage,
and time release of its data. The project enhanced the capacity of BBS in collecting, managing, and disseminating data,
promoting a user-centric enabling environment to make relevant data accessible for evidence-based policymaking and
other uses. The project’s objectives were realistic, and the technical innovation proposed were aligned with the delivery
of the first-ever digital census and introduction of the CAPI tool which greatly contributed to data quality through real-
time field monitoring.

88.     The risk assessment during the project preparation and implementation identified mitigation measures, which
proved highly relevant. Particular attention was paid to GoB’s political support and the risk of duplication of efforts and
investments by several DP’s financing vertical projects in BBS. The project’s components and activities were also aligned
with the country’s development objectives and statistical needs. Although there were delays at the start of the project,
particularly in setting up the PIU and hiring of the PIU staff and the TP, the project was able to support skills development
to key project staff through retraining on project management, FM, and procurement.

89.     Therefore, the quality at entry of the World Bank is rated Satisfactory.


Quality of Supervision

Rating: Satisfactory

90.     The World Bank’s implementation support role was to facilitate effective project implementation and ensure
value for money, transparency, and accountability. The Bank team provided close supervision to the project
implementation. The TTL and other key project team members were also based in the country and therefore provided
closer supervision and support to the project PIU and the implementing agency.

91.      The project team also conducted regular supervision and implementation support missions. As evidenced by
the series of 12 project ISRs produced over the project’s life, the task team conducted regular supervision missions and
worked to resolve the issues which were raised. There were at least three implementation support mission per year during
the project implementation (2018-2024) and one MTR mission. Efforts were made by the task team adapted to changing
circumstances brough about by the COVID-19 pandemic and conducted virtual supervision missions to maintain support
for the project implementation through the crisis period.

92.     Therefore, the quality supervision of the World Bank during the implementation and support is rated Satisfactory.

 RISK TO DEVELOPMENT OUTCOME

 94.     The main risk to maintaining development results of the NSS from the start of the project to closure are linked
 to GoB’s political support to provide the right enabling environment through the necessary legal, administrative, and
 budgetary framework. Project successfully navigated any loss in political capital by being transparent on methodology to
 produce statistics, making the methodology and data available in the public domain, and through overall strengthening
 of BBS.

 95.      Risks relating to institutional capacity for implementation and sustainability, as well as fiduciary were
 Substantial. The various project activities enhanced HR capacity and stewardship within BBS to tackle the challenges
 of day-to-day work. Project undertook the time recruitment and training of FM staff and developed a comprehensive,
 project specific FM manual as part of the operational manual for effectiveness. The intensive activities designed and
 implemented through the support of the Twinning Partner will also ensure at least in the medium term the demand and
 use of statistics. The gains made will however be continued through the support of the government core statistical
 activities and reforms as proposed under the draft NSDS 2024-2030, and the proposed public sector operation which
 include a large component on data that focus on improving the capacity of NSS and BBS.

 96.     On stakeholder risk comprising on the duplication of Development Partner (DP) financing, the PIU through the
 BBS management organized regular meetings with key development partners around major statistical activities . Some
 of the major activities around which DPs coordinated efforts include the Population and Housing Census, HIES, LFS, etc.



V. LESSONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

97.      One of the lessons learned from the implementation of the NSDS ISP is the importance of close collaboration
between the World Bank team, the PIU, BBS authority and the SID on the successful attainment of outcomes and
address the unexpected challenges during the project implementation. Although the NSDS ISP project encountered
delays at the start, it has made considerable contributions which was only possible as the project quickly adapted and
addressed delays accession by COVID-19 pandemic, utilization of new and innovative technologies in data collection,
overcoming some key institutional delays and adapting to change in government advisory in relation to foreign trainings
by shifting to local subject matter trainings. One other key lesson from the implementation of NSDS ISP is that the quality
and reliable data needs are large and deeply rooted in Bangladesh. Thus, support to BBS to improve the overall capacity
is a long-term commitment and warrants a phased approach.

98.      Main recommendation for future operations is that the ownership and the role of the PIU need to be
strengthened and strategically situated within the NSO. The PIU therefore needs to be empowered and the Wings fully
involved and supported through eligible budget with an internal prior approval mechanism in place based on workplans
which does not delay planned activities. There is also need for any future operation to continue the momentum and focus
on skills, modernization of physical, statistical, and ICT infrastructure both at national and sub-national levels to enhance
capacity of the NSO and the NSS.

ANNEX 8. Selected significant Activities Carried Out Under the Project

   •    A large-scale Consultation workshop was organized on February 24, 2019, titled "Bangladesh: Role of Statistics
        towards the Development Journey".
   •    Launching Workshops were organized on 23rd June 2019 at Pan Pacific Sonargaon Hotel and on 24th June 2019 at
        the conference room of BBS.
   •    Under this project, the Statistics Act, 2013, translated into English (Statistics Act, 2013) which was gazette on
        September 08, 2020.
   •    During Covid Pandemic - the Perception Survey on Livelihood 2020 was conducted from 13 September 2020 to 19
        September 2020 with the support of NSDS-ISP
   •    A high-level Council ‘National Advisory Council on Statistics (NACS)’ was formed.
   •    Prepare and publish BBS Glossary (Concepts and Definitions) with 2041 standardized concepts and definitions.
   •    Two rounds of User Satisfaction Survey (USS) were conducted (Base line in 2022 and End line in 2024).
   •    Financial and technical assistance were provided to Household Income & Expenditure Survey (HIES) 2022, Labor
        Force Survey (LFS) 2022, Population and Housing census 2022 and background work of National Population Register
        (NPR).
   •    Pre-test and Piloting activities of Annual Crop Production Survey (ACPS), Health and Morbidity Status Survey
        (HMSS) and Survey of Manufacturing (SMI) were conducted.
   •    Four Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) were signed between Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) and
        Bangladesh Bank, Finance Division, National Board of Revenue (NBR) and Federation of Bangladesh Chambers of
        Commerce and Industry (FBCCI) to enrich the statistical system and to establish institutional relationship.
