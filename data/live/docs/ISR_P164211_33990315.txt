 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Good progress has been made in implementing project activities since the project's restructuring and the last mission of the Bank in June 2022,

  motivating an overall progress rating of Satisfactory (S), which is an upgrade from Moderately Satisfactory (MS). It is noted, however, that

  component 2 of the project (developing tourism sites and destinations) trails the progress of other project components, most in part due to the

  delayed start of select contracts, such as the contract for the design of Cape Coast Castle and the Protected Areas (Kakum, Mole, Shai Hills). The

  project has made satisfactory progress towards achieving the project development objective (PDO), as all PDO-level indicators have made varying

  progress towards their respective end targets.

  Component 1: Strengthening the tourism enabling environment: there have been several positive developments. These include the roll-out of

  capacity building training to key industry stakeholders, ongoing international and domestic marketing campaigns, and ongoing training of trainers of

  Lower-Level Tourism and Hospitality skills. Training on the tourism sector's regulations targeted 150 private sector providers and other workshops

  and training events for industry players including tour guides, taxi drivers, artists etc. was recently completed.

  Component 2: Developing tourism sites and destinations. Several tourist sites and destinations have now received infrastructural upgrades and

  developments, including the Kintampo Waterfalls, Bonwire Crafts Village, Bogyawe Palace Museum—Akwamufie, Ankasa Forest Reserve

  Receptive Facility, Nzulezu Receptive Facility and Gushegu Smock Village. Notably, the development of the Kwame Nkrumah Memorial Park was

  40% complete by early December 2022; this work is expected to be completed by end-March 2023.

  Component 3: Tourism enterprise support program: The second round of grants under MOTAC's administration was successfully implemented

  within the projected timeframe; as such, the US$10m reallocated to the grants program in June 2022 is now fully committed, with 85% (US$8.5m)

  already disbursed to grant beneficiaries, while the outstanding 15% (US$1.5m) expected to be disbursed in January 2023. A total of 1,249 grants

  were approved under the different windows (CRGs, COVID recovery grants (1002), SMEGs, SME grants (206) and SUGs, site upgrade grants

  (41)); out of which 55% of the grant beneficiaries were females.
