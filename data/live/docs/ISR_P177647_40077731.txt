3. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 3.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
   As the program entered its fourth year of implementation, key activities are progressing, and achievements supported
   through the operation include:
       1. Implementation of Recovery and Accelerated Learning (ReAL) plan. The ReAL plan has been approved and
           implemented in 250 LGs with intensive support. An external evaluation of ReAL’s effectiveness is underway and is
           expected to provide feedback and recommendations on best practices for learning outcomes to inform scale-up
           and institutionalization.
       2. Support for implementation of Integrated curriculum (IC). During the first three years, nearly 500 LGs have
           received additional support to strengthen IC implementation in early grades. Support included provision of
           additional learning materials for students, teacher guides, and customized teacher training. At least one teacher
           from each schools from these 500 LGs received the customized IC training. Given persistent low learning levels,
           government should ensure that all the remaining early grade teachers receive IC training and prioritize
           complementary activities to improve student learning.

       3. Teacher mentoring program: The Standard Operating Procedure and Guideline (SOPG) for the mentoring
          program was approved and the program has been rolled out in 150 LGs in Years 2 and 3, with and additional 100
          LGs planned for this year. The program targets new teachers and has been well-received by both mentee and
          mentors, but requires modifications to improve sustainability and scalability.
       4. Pro-poor targeted scholarship (PPTS): The PPTS targeting formula using Proxy Means Testing (PMT) has been
          updated using the most recent National Living Standards Survey data. The updated scheme has been rolled out in
          a phased manner at the basic and secondary levels, starting from Grades 6 and 9. Coverage and administration
          have improved year-on-year; however, additional efforts are needed to ensure all LGs and eligible students are
          aware of the scheme and able to apply.
  Citizen Engagement. The MTR and the 2025 JRM for the SESP invited representatives from local and provincial
  governments, teacher associations, School Management Committee associations, and civil society organizations (CSOs),
  including the National Campaign for Education in Nepal (NCE-N) and the Association of INGOs in Nepal—Education
  Working Group (AIN-EWG). These stakeholders shared best practices, contributions, challenges, and constraints in
  accessing quality education, as well as potential ways forward. The biannual review meetings provide a platform for
  broader stakeholder engagement and an opportunity to reinforce the importance of ongoing dialogue between the
  government and stakeholders, while also enabling reflection on how CSOs can contribute to achieving the targets and
  commitments outlined in the SESP.
