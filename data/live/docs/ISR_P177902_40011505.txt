4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
  This ISR is based on the sixth implementation review and support of the Nepal: Accelerating Transport and Trade
  Connectivity in Eastern South Asia (ACCESS) – Nepal Phase 1 Project, conducted from March 18-April 1, 2025.
   The Project is making good progress in the transport component where the civil works contracts for Butwal-Gurusinghe
  sections, since its award in October 2024, has achieved 7.4% physical progress. The pre-construction activities such as,
  establishment of camp sites, full mobilization of plant and equipment, and obtaining of other licenses and permits, have
  been completed, and activities related to relocation of utilities and tree cutting, are nearing completion. The contracts
  have suffered from initial health and safety issues and work has commenced before the formal approval of the
  Contractor's ESMP, that has since been corrected. The procurement process for the CG road (19 km) and Tinahu bridge
  works are expected to commence soon, with the DCID meeting the bid document submission deadline to the Bank of
  April 30, 2025.
  The trade component under the Ministry of Industry, Commerce, and Supplies (MoICS), has been experiencing slow
  progress. The procurement process for the Integrated Border Management System (IBMS) under the Intermodal
  Transport Development Board (NITDB) is ongoing, while the activity under the Department of Customs (DoC) remains
  stagnant due to delays in identifying the procurement entity. MoICS will conduct regular monitoring of the activities
  among the Implementing Units to expedite the overall implementation progress of the component. The green corridor
  component being implemented by Department of Forest and Social Conservation (DoFSC) under the Ministry of Forest
  and Environment (MoFE) has been slow. The review assessed that the component requires further assessments in order
  to ensure the correct interventions considering climate resilience more holistically. Given the available timeline for
  implementation of this component that would fall short in time, it has been agreed to discontinue from this project scope
  that will be formalized through the restructuring of the project. Nevertheless, the project will continue to support
  resilience through several other interventions in the development of the roads, as part of the project’s development
  objective.
   The E&S implementation performance has been downgraded to Moderately Satisfactory (MS) due to the
  commencement of works without full approval of the contractor ESMPs and the need for labor camp management and
  health and safety measure enhancement . The IP rating remains MS despite DCID’s request for an upgrade, given the
  notable progress. IP Ratings will be reassessed for upgradation upon the achievement of the following: (i) submission of
  the Bid Documents on the GC section and Tinau bridge by April 30, 2025, and (ii) Project restructuring. Considering the
  progress on key activities, performance, and identified actions to advance implementation, the likelihood of achieving the
  PDO is retained as “Satisfactory”.

  The ACCESS MPA Program was approved by the Board on June 28, 2022. The Nepal and Bangladesh Phase 1 Projects
  became effective on February 10, 2023, and May 3, 2023, respectively. While project implementation has started to pick
  up pace, both projects lag the original schedule due to the extensive time delay between World Bank Board approval and
  project effectiveness. The ACCESS Phase 2, providing assistance to the Royal Government of Bhutan (RGoB) has just been
  approved by the Board of the World Bank on May 1, 2025. The ACCESS program regional coordination and peer-to-peer
  learning platform has been launched with several stakeholder coordination and knowledge sharing events (physical and
  virtual) organized.
