 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Following are the key findings of the BIMP-II based on the ninth implementation support review, conducted from August 28 to September 13, 2023.

  Overall Performance. The review noted the hybrid Program is making considerable progress in achieving majority of the PforR DLIs targets except

  for the slow progressing DLI-3 and -7. The PforR program so far has disbursed US$61.9 million with US$5 million in the pipeline and approx.

  US$11.26 million under third party verification for the disbursement. On the other hand, the overall progress of Investment Project Financing (IPF)

  components is lagging with disbursement of US$0.27 million against the allocation of US$9.15 million. Furthermore, the review assessed approx.

  US$6 million of IPF funds are unlikely to be disbursed by the closure date. Accordingly, the Implementing Agency (Bridge Branch, BB) is

  considering to propose a third restructuring shortly after assessing options to either (i) reallocate a portion of the IPF funds to the better

  performing PforR components or, (ii) proactively cancel the funds that is expected not to be utilized. Considering the Program is closing in

  less than a year and still US$52 million remains to be disbursed, the Program needs to accelerate the overall implementation progress to timely

  utilize the remaining funds. The Program team appreciates the National Planning Commission’s (NPC) proactive role in providing smooth, effective,

  and timely verification of the DLIs. Similarly, the Program team appreciates the role of the National Vigilance Centre (NVC) in auditing and issuing

  Program Non-Conformance Reports as needed.Considering the overall progress, the Implementation Progress and likelihood of achievement of the

  Project Development Objective (PDO) is retained as ‘Moderately Satisfactory’.

  Program for Results (PforR) performance. The overall progress of the PforR component is on track. The DLI-1 is near or will exceed the revised

  target of 5,000 meters (m) as 796 m are under NPC verification. For DLI-2, while there this is 1,029 m worth of Bridge safety upgrades currently

  under NPC verification, the remaining target of 2,776 m is at different stages of implementation and to be submitted to NPC for verification by May

  15, 2024. For DLI-3, the Program achieved 1,715 m so far with 720 m submitted for NPC verification and 3,148 m are under implementation. Tree

  felling for 11 bridges (approx. 910m) is foreseen to be a challenge, and two options were explored to address this potential shortfall in achieving

  DLI-3 targets. For DLI-5, 850 m were submitted for NPC verification on September 3, 2023, thus achieving the revised target of 7,300 m. Under

  DLI-6, the Standard bidding document (SBD) for Design and Build (DB) contract, have been verified and disbursed with the endorsement of MoPIT

  Secretary and submission to PPMO for approval. For DLI-7, the BB submitted 1,549 entries for NPC verification. The above DLIs progress

  translated into the BB submitting US$11.26 million request for third party verification, dated Sept. 3, 2023 (DLI 3- US$ 6.35 m, DLI 5-US$ 3.74 m

  and DLI 7- US$ 1.16 m).

  Investment Project Financing (IPF) performance. The overall progress of the IPF components have been slow. For IPF-1, comments on the

  technical proposal evaluation for the feasibility study and detailed design for upgrading of a priority section of the East-West-Highway from

  Chanauta to Gaddachauki are yet to be addressed by the BB. The technical proposal received for the ESIA is under evaluation by the BB team. It is

  to note that should the BB proceed with the award of consultancy contracts under this IPF, these consultancies will take place after the Program

  closing date. Accordingly, those payments cannot be processed under the Program and will have to be borne by the Government of Nepal. Under

  IPF-2, the BB completed corrective actions for 55 no. of NCR during FY2021/22 and total 59 no. of NCR in the FY2022/23. For IPF-3, the BB team

  identified four bridges (Narayani, Bijaypur, Bagmati and flyover in Balkhu junction) for advance bridge design, among which the consultation for

  Narayani bridge has been conducted. The preliminary study of these bridges is expected to be completed by September 2023, and the design of

  two bridges (Narayani and Bijaypur), is expected to be completed by the Program closing date. under IPF-4, approx. 978 participants were trained

  during 38 events of various capacity development activities. Under IPF-5, the process for hiring Procurement and Contract Management Individual

  Consultant, Capacity Development individual Consultant is due for completion by September 2023. The consultant for updating inventory for the

  Bridge Management System (BMS) is expected to be onboard by November 5, 2023. Under IPF-6, the Design and Advanced Technology Cell

  Comments

  Following are the explanation for the risks that are categorized as substantial:

  Political and Governance risks: Substantial. Nepal’s political situation remains uncertain with a fragile coalition government impacting both the

  federal and provincial levels of government. The federal system provides opportunities to decentralize development benefits and make service

  delivery more effective and accountable. Nevertheless, the risks of political uncertainty, jurisdictional overlap between the three tiers of government,

  and need for more clarity and coherence between policies and devolved powers will remain in the coming period. While the extent to which political

  risks can be mitigated through project-specific measures is limited, the World Bank will maintain continuity in policy dialogue with the government,

  and project design will include specific actions to build public support through broader stakeholder participation, strategic communication, and

  outreach. Accordingly, the overall political and governance risk and its potential impact on the Program activities is rated as substantial.

  Macroeconomic: Substantial. The forecast is subject to multiple downside risks. An erratic monsoon and natural disasters could dampen

  agricultural growth. Political uncertainty could hold back private investment and delay public capital expenditures. A renewed spike in commodity

  prices or continued food export bans by India would raise prices and widen the current account deficit. Higher inflation could keep policy rates

  elevated, increase domestic debt servicing costs, and drag on growth. Delays in pivoting fiscal revenues away from import taxation and towards

  domestic revenue sources would leave fiscal revenues vulnerable to swings in trade flows. Accordingly, the overall macroeconomic risk and its

  influence on the Program is retained as Substantial.

  Fiduciary: Substantial. The residual fiduciary risk under the Program continues to remain substantial. The risk stems from the potential fraud and

  corruption issues likely to face the Program, which could potentially impede achievement of development objectives. For bridge works, key concerns

  relate to collusion or manipulation of bidding processes, fraudulent claims for payment against quantity items, deficient quality combined with

  inappropriate payments for completed works, and erroneous contract variations. To mitigate these risks, the Program has placed

  additional strengthening measures through the Program Action Plan. These include: (a) verification of Bridge Dossiers and physical inspection of

  Program bridges to trigger disbursements, (b) technical auditing by the NVC and subsequent referral of serious non‐ compliances to Nepal’s

  Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority, (c) use of the BSMS which offers enhanced means of quality control on remote worksites.

  Environment and Social: Substantial. The residual risk continues to remain Substantial. There are various potential E&S risks associated with

  the Program such as, vulnerability of works to natural disasters and climate change, potential exposure to child labor, and labor influx including GBV

  and risk of SEA. To mitigate these risks, the Program Actions Plans has been devised with the multi‐tiered approach, that is being followed by

  the implementing agency, as a part of the legal covenants, while implementing the Program. The key implementation support plan also includes a

  pillar on labor risk management focus on monitoring enforcement of contract provisions relating to labor camps, workers’ conduct, prohibition of

  child labor, and access to resources for communities that may be affected.

  Overall: Considering the ratings of various risks outlined above, the overall residual risks remains Substantial.
