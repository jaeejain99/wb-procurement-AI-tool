 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Since the last Implementation Support Mission in December 2019, the SAHEP project has made progress on the following milestones: (i) the

  Subsidiary Agreements were signed off by the Ministry of Finance and the on-lending contracts were signed with Vietnam Development Bank’s

  transaction office; (ii) Vietnam National University of Agriculture (VNUA) and Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST) have signed

  contracts worth of $18 million and made the first two withdrawal applications from the IDA credit for a total of $3,805,730 for advanced contract

  payment; (iii) Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City (IUH) Pre-Feasibility Study report (Pre-FS) was approved on April 6, 2020 and National

  Economics University’s (NEU) revised Feasibility Study (FS) was approved by Ministry of Education and Training (MOET) in June 2020. The five

  implementing agencies (IAs) have been proactively using their counterpart funds to carry out activities on enhancing research, teaching and

  management capacity to achieve result indicator targets in an overall satisfactory manner.

  However, major delays in IUH’s resettlement will likely result in an incomplete campus construction by the project closing date. No activities of

  resettlement and site clearance have been implemented since December 2019, pending the approval of the 1/500 planning map by the Ho Chi Minh

  City’s People Committee (HCMC PC) and the approval of the FS for the campus by MOIT. The Bank team’s assessment is that the project will not

  be able to meet its project development objectives and therefore affirm that the project needs to be restructured to cancel the civil works component

  of the IUH. The Bank team has communicated with the government the necessity to cancel the US$22.5 million of the IUH’s civil work plus US$13.9

  million returned funding for VAT, and revision of the project results indicators and targets. The project restructuring should be completed by

  December 31, 2020.
