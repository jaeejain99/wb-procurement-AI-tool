 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Status

  Component One: Strengthening Organizational Performance. This component is rated Moderately Satisfactory. The three institutions [Office of

  the Head of Civil Service OHCS), Public Services Commission (PSC) and the Commission on Human Rights and Administrative

  Justice(CHRAJ)] under the component have progressed in implementing project activities. With the enhancements in processes, procedures and

  systems including the supply of IT equipment, the OHCS is now conducting recruitment and promotion interviews online for applicants and staff

  irrespective of applicant’s/staff member's location in the country. The use of the online system has increased the efficiency at the OHCS and

  reduced the burden of traveling for applicants and civil servants to attend interviews. To enhance improved service delivery, the project supported a

  strengthened performance assessment system to evaluate the performance of civil servants. An improved template for Client Service Charters

  (CSC) were developed and used to revise the CSCs for five civil service institutions including the OHCS. The CSCs have been published on the

  website of the institutions.

  The installation of video conferencing systems and peer-learning has enhanced the performance of the PSC. Meetings are now being held virtually

  with agencies and public services under the PSC. Institutional gender action plans with scorecards have been developed and under implementation

  by the PSC and four selected entities (Ghana Immigration Service, Ghana Health Service, Lands Commission and the Drivers and Vehicles

  Licensing Authority). Client service charters (CSCs) were revised, and staff trained for the above listed institutions in addition to the Social Security

  and National Insurance Trust (SSNIT). The revised CSCs have been published on the website of the institutions. A report on the review of the

  strengthening the grievance redress and complaints handling procedures of CHRAJ was adopted and in use.

  Component Two: Strengthening Selected Entities’ Performance. This component is rated Moderately Satisfactory. A business process

  reengineering and queue management system have been developed for the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Authority (DVLA). Administrative Caveat

  was developed to boost death registration for the Births and Death Registry (BDR). An agreement was reached between the Ghana Health Service

  and the BDR to collaborate, revise and use the existing "Maternal Health Booklets" to support the birth registration efforts of the BDR. A framework

  for the institution of a national innovation challenge (NIC) competition was developed for the Ministry of Environment, Science, Technology and

  Innovation (MESTI). The NIC was launched in October 2023 and the first level of review completed in November 2023. The technical committee has

  recommended 100 applications to be further screened with presentations on each application. MESTI has also established a collaboration with the

  South African Department of Science & Technology to catalyze innovations in the public sector. An independent review of the administrative

  structures, procedures and policies of the Passport Office which is a business process review was completed.

  At the Passport Office, implementation of the recommendations from the business process review is yielding results. Positive achievements include

  the reduction of backlog of applications from 60,000 to 7,000 as of November 30, 2023, following the acquisition of IT equipment to support the

  vetting of received applications and biometric capture of applicants. The delays by the supplier and the supply of the two chip-embedded printers

  which were below the technical specifications stated in the contract adversely affected the progress of the activities, most notably the reduction of

  backlogs of printed passports.

  Component Three: Improving Monitoring and Evaluation: This component is rated Satisfactory. The draft National M&E Policy was submitted to

  the Cabinet and received advice to deepen collaboration with the National Development Planning Commission (NDPC). Peer review of the

  functional processes, structures, and practices of Policy Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Departments (PPMEDs) of selected Ministries,

  Departments and Agencies (MDAs) and Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies (MMDAs) has been completed.

  Component Four: Project Management, Coordination and Public Reporting: This component is rated Moderately Satisfactory. Project

  Management and coordination has improved. Two project quarterly meetings were held. The Project Management Unit (PMU) prepared a semi-

  annual report in August 2023. The M&E specialist of the project has collected data, analyzed them and presented the data sets to the IVA for

  verification. However, the external audit report was submitted in August 2023, two clear months after the June deadline.

  Way forward:

  The Bank Team and component leads will continue to follow up with selected entities (SEs) through bi-weekly meetings to resolve any

  implementation issues that may arise.
