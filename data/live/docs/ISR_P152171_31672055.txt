 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project is now progressing well and has some notable achievements including having supported the establishment of the State Interests and

  Governance Authority (SIGA).

  Component 1: Progress has been relatively slow to date – the most important output has been a consultancy to design the specifications for a

  BI/DW for the GRA. Work on contracting the BI/DW has not yet started, due to concerns about the high costs of the proposed solution. The WB

  team has agreed with the GRA team on an action plan to:

          Review the scope of functionalities to be covered by the BI/BW;

            Reduce the cost element and finalize the bidding documents before end of November 2019.

            Launch the procurement to finalize the selection process before March 2020.

            Hold regular meetings (every three weeks) via Video Conference to support implementation of the component

  The WB team recommended using the existing BI tools to start correlating data from their databases to help the auditors in the conduct of their

  activities. The current results indicator for this component of the project (Tax audit yield ratio) does not have a clear methodology and will be

  replaced by increase Tax Revenue ratio to GDP, with respect to the base in 2018, by 3% annually by through increasing Compliance and quality of

  Audits.

  Component 2: The TDMD has made considerable progress in strengthening its capacity, developing and implementing Ghana’s Medium-Term

  Debt Management Strategy (MTDS). The main issues are: i) the establishment of a trading room has not been completed due to difficulties in

  setting up a dedicated internet connection for which the service provider needs help from the IT Unit of the MOF; and ii) the Division needs support

  and potential additional funds for scanning legal agreements to establish an off-site backup and recovery center. These issues require urgent

  attention from the Management of the MoF.

  Component 3: Considerable progress has been made in developing the necessary manuals, guidelines, Human Resource (HR) and long-term

  institutional training capacity. It is important to ensure that this work becomes institutionally embedded. This will require tha t:

            the draft PIM Regulations are finalized as soon as possible and approved by Parliament.

            Capacity building activities be strengthened with more ‘customized’ training programs.

            a medium to long-term plan on establishing a PIM database using the IBP (Integrated Bank for Projects) and connecting it to the Hyperion

             be developed.

            linkage between Public Investment and Asset Division (PIAD) and National Development Planning Commission (NDPC) work be

             strengthened with more specific mandates integrating the roles of both in PIM planning, appraisal, selection, and implementation.

            more focus should be on enforcing the PIM Regulations and guidelines/manuals on large-scale projects in the key MDAs, and then extend

             to other projects in sub-national governments, SOEs, others.

  Component 4: : This component has supported the major achievement of the establishment of the State Interest and Governance (SIGA[AK1] ).

  Even though the Board has been constituted, there is still the need to satisfy the covenants of (i) putting in place senior management (with terms of

  reference, qualification and experience, satisfactory to the Association); as well as (ii) a technical procurement and financial management

  assessment of said. Currently, a consultant (KPMG) has been procured to conduct a corporate HR review (report expected in February 2020); and

  are expected to also facilitate recruitments in positions where gaps are identified. Until then, resources allocated to subcomponent 4.2 may not be

  disbursed. It is important that the Equity Study financed by this component is formally approved by Cabinet and that State Ownership Policy

  document is concluded soon

  Component 5: Coordination of the project has been very good. The main challenge for project implementation have been delays of internal MoF

  processes (particularly in the case of procurement). Also, the implementation of the GEMS-TA Project has increased the workload of some

  implementing divisions of the MoF which tends to delay some deliverables.

  Procurement: The Procurement Unit has been doing good work, but delays have occurred in procurement processes due to internal administrative

  requirements of the Ministry, for example, the legal review of contracts.

  Financial management: The WB team has requested a variance analysis report which provides systemic reasons for variances between actual and

  budgeted expenditure. This report is expected to make the IFRs more meaningful and strengthen the project’s budgetary control system. The WB

  team stressed the importance of MoF complying with the timelines in the FM action plan when providing missing documentation.
