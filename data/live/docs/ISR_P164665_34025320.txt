 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Status

            Since the project restructuring, project activities are now focused on achieving tangible results as per the revised performance based

             conditions (PBC) matrix and technical notes that were earlier prepared and agreed upon.

            Significant progress has since been made in designing and implementing specific interventions to achieve those results. Subsequently, 35

             contracts have been signed, 15 concept notes have been reviewed and cleared, 9 concept notes are under review and 22 contracts will be

             signed by March 2023. As a result, the Project has disbursed 42 percent of funds at the time of this ICR.

            Project coordination has been actively engaged with the selected entities and arranged for Bank missions, quarterly progress review

             workshops, and organize project steering committee meetings.

            Adherence to the Standard Operating Procedures for approving consultants’ outputs and payments has brought about transparency in the

             process and led to improved quality of outputs. Technical evaluation committees (TEC) have been meeting to review outputs of

             consultants and approve final deliverables.

            An independent verification agent (IVA) has since been contracted and has concluded its first verification of the PBCs as of September 30,

             2022. US$2.00 million is expected to be disbursed under the PBC component as verified in the first report.

  Component One: Strengthening Organizational Performance. The three institutions [Office of the Head of Civil Service OHCS), Public Services

  Commission (PSC) and the Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice(CHRAJ)] under the component have progressed in

  implementing project activities. A report on strengthening the grievance redress and complaints handling mechanism was prepared and

  adopted. Two separate draft reports on the rapid institutional assessment for the PSC and the OHCS were prepared and the reports are under

  review. The quality of concept notes and terms of reference improved over the period. The Technical Evaluation Committees at both the PSC and

  the OHCS have been active in reviewing the outputs of the consultants when they are delivered. The Project supported the OHCS in improving the

  performance assessment process in the Civil Service by reviewing the assessment templates to make them focused and targeted in ensuring

  compliance and improving service delivery.

  Component Two: Strengthening Selected Entities’ Performance. A business process reengineering and queue management system have been

  developed for the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Authority (DVLA). Administrative Caveat was developed to boost death registration for the Births and

  Death Registry (BDR). An agreement was reached between the Ghana Health Service and the BDR to collaborate, revise and use the existing

  "Maternal Health Booklets" to support the birth registration efforts of the BDR. A framework for the institution of a national innovation challenge

  competition was developed for the Ministry of Environment, Science, Technology and Innovation (MESTI). MESTI has also established a

  collaboration with the South African Department of Science & Technology to catalyze innovations in the public sector. An independent review of the

  administrative structures, procedures and policies of the Passport Office which is a business process review is nearing completion.

  Component Three: Improving Monitoring and Evaluation: The Monitoring and Evaluation Secretariat (MES) conducted an annual citizens'

  survey (referred to as Listening to Ghana) and evaluated some flagship programs. Going forward, the MES will now be focusing on the following

  activities within the next eight months - study on M&E practice, strengthening public sector M&E through peer-to-peer learning, organizing results

  fairs in selected regions.

  Component Four: Project Management, Coordination and Public Reporting: Since restructuring, Project Management and coordination has

  improved. Two project quarterly meetings were held. The Project Management Unit (PMU) coordinated the preparation of annual work plans and

  procurement plans that were approved in December 2023. An independent verification agent was procured and a first verification report for the

  project was prepared. A second report is expected to be delivered by March 31, 2023. The annual project progress report is to be produced by

  March 31, 2023.

  Way forward:

  The Bank team has approved a final list of activities, including work to be outsourced for which the contracts will be signed latest by March 31,

  2023. For inhouse interventions and non-consulting services, concept notes are to be prepared and be approved by the same date.

  The Bank Team and component leads will continue to follow up SEs through bi-weekly meetings to resolve any implementation issues that may

  arise.

  The Bank team will continue to work with the Government of Ghana to ensure all necessary actions are taken with due consideration given to the

  project closing date of Dec 31, 2023.
