 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  During the second year of implementation, Kenya has experienced extreme events including two flood cycles, a landslide, locust invasion and

  COVID19. This occasioned the Government of Kenya (GoK) to make a partial disbursement of resources from the Cat DDO instrument in

  December 2019 (USD$70 million), with the balance disbursed in May 2020 (USD$113 million).

  Linked to the Cat DDO Pillar 1, the World Bank leveraged Technical Assistance (TA) resources (US$470K) from the African, Caribbean, and

  Pacific (ACP) – European Union (EU) Natural Disaster Risk Reduction (NDRR) Program for “Strengthening the Government of Kenya’s (GoK)’s

  institutional, planning and policy frameworks to manage climate and disaster risk”. The Bank-Executed grant financed activities support four

  components: (i) mainstreaming DRM; (ii) enhancing emergency response capacity; (iii) strengthening the legal framework for DRM; and (iv)

  fortifying the regulatory framework for the built environment in Kenya. For carrying out activities under components (i), (ii) and (iii), the Japanese

  firm Nippon Koei Co., LTD was hired for June 2019-July 2020 and worked with the National Disaster Operations Center (NDOC) and National

  Drought Management Authority (NDMA) as the main Government Stakeholders. Nippon Koei’s consultancy activities included:

  i. updating the methodology that has been used by the National Drought Management Authority (NDMA) for the development of County Multi-

  Hazard Risk Profiles,

  ii. revising the National Emergency Response Plan (2009),

  iii. preparing a Guidance Note for the formulation of County Level Emergency Operation Plans, and,

  iv. supporting the advancement of an approved Disaster Risk Management (DRM) Legal Framework, i.e., the DRM Policy and Act, through the

  National Assembly.

  All the components were completed, and the firm handed over the final version of all the four documents in July 2020. It is important to note that

  the first three deliverables underwent pubic participation as provided for in the Constitution of Kenya (2010) and this process was led jointly by

  GoK officials drawn from NDOC and National Treasury.
