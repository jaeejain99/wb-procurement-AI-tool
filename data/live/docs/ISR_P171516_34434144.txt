3. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 3.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
   1. The NEHEP Program strategically supports a sub-set of the Government National Higher Education Program (NHEP) of
   US$1.15 billion for six years including the base year (2021). The amount includes US$60 million IDA Credit. The IDA Credit
   has two components: Component 1 (US$ 57 million) uses for Program for Results and Component 2 (US$3 million) is
   Investment Project Financing (IPF). The PforR supports through Disbursement Linked Indicators - DLIs under four Results
   Areas clustered around six DLIs and 18 disbursement linked results (DLRs).

   2. PDO Performance. The Program Development Objectives (PDOs) are 'to strengthen labor market relevance and quality
   of higher education, boost collaborative research and innovation, and enhance equitable access for underprivileged and
   disaster affected groups.' Good progress has been observed in the implementation of research, entrepreneurship,
   performance-based grants and supporting Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in lagging/ remote regions with ‘equity
   grants’, support to underprivileged students, and share of students in online teaching-learning, under Results Areas 1, 2,
   3 and 4. These contribute to meet the PDO Outcome 2 (to boost collaborative research and innovation) and Outcome 3

  (to enhance equitable access for underprivileged and disaster affected groups). Implementation of Labor Market Driven
  Programs (LMDP) and student enrollment in these programs is making modest progress during the last 4-5 months
  contributing to PDO Outcome 1 (to strengthen labor-market relevance and quality of higher education) and PDO
  Indicators 1 and 2.

  3. The targeted actions jointly launched by the implementing agency with universities and HEIs in close association and
  facilitation of the World Bank team helped the universities and Deans’ offices to align their new/revised academic
  programs with labor market relevance. Five LMDP have been introduced. UGC has provided technical inputs to 12 new
  LMDPs under Kathmandu, Pokhara and Mid-West universities to finalize and resubmit for approval by January 15, 2024.
  Additional 9 LMDPs are in immediate pipeline to complete and UGC approval by March 15, 2025. Other 18 LMDPs (6 from
  TU Institute of Forestry and 12 from Institute of Engineering) would be completed by June 30, 2025. Faculty training
  contributing to expedite the implementation of PDO outcome 1 and indicators 1 and 2 is also making good progress. 530
  Faculties trained and additional faculty groups formed under different universities for continuation. Demand for faculty
  training significantly increased from the faculties including through peer learning. This is also contributing to the
  implementation of LMDPs. Based on the progress made on most of the PDO indicators and actions agreed, the PDO
  performance rating continues to be ‘Satisfactory’.

  4. Implementation Progress Performance continues to be ‘Moderately Satisfactory’. There has been additional
  improvement in the implementation. Recently appointed new leaders of all universities are proactively involved in the
  implementation of the program including towards sustaining the earlier reforms – UGC has allocated 60 merit-based
  faculty positions to be recruited by interested universities. UGC and universities are closely following up in maintaining
  universities academic calendar. The UGC revitalized the UGC-TU Program Coordination Committee, and enhanced
  University Coordination Committee of Vice Chancellors with improved interactions/ university visits. New accreditation
  and research directors with proven track records in quality assurance and accreditation (QAA) and research recruited.
  However, there is still staffing gap for administrative support in research and accreditation divisions to facilitate extended
  activities of entrepreneurship under research division, and program and university accreditation and quality
  enhancement under QAA division. UGC agreed to hire four officer level staff – two each for research and QAA divisions in
  3-4 months time. To expedite M&E and Reporting of safeguard activities including capacity building of UGC and
  beneficiary institution, hiring of 2 safeguard consultants is at the final stage. The consultants will work closely with the
  functioning safeguard committee of UGC. To address the staffing gap in various divisions/sections, UGC will continue
  engaging six Master level student- interns. UGC has also hired three consulting firms to strengthen EMIS, to carry out 2nd
  round beneficiary satisfaction survey, and 2nd round fiduciary review. UGC in consultation with MOEST and Ministry of
  Finance is planning to hire four officer level staff (two for research/ entrepreneurship and two for accreditation) to
  expedite implementation, monitoring, documentation, dissemination, and to enhance visibility of key reforms.

  5. Citizen Engagement, Gender and Climate Change. UGC has been regularly organizing consultations with stakeholders,
  especially with universities, campus management team, students, teachers, alumnae and other stakeholders. Among
  others, these include information dissemination and facilitation of universities/HEIs and individuals in strengthening
  higher education system including quality assurance and accreditation, quality enhancement (QE), digitalization, and
  widening access to quality education for disadvantaged students. Different stakeholders participated in the citizen
  engagement include Principals and FM experts (around 130 people) from 54 campuses participation in 5-day higher
  education program as an annual event; key leaders of all universities (Vice Chancellors, Rectors, Registrars; total 22
  people) participation in a half-day leadership sensitization workshop; participation of around 680 key stakeholders from 6
  HEIs in QAA peer reviews and post peer reviews; participation of around 80 technical staff of accredited HEIs in QE
