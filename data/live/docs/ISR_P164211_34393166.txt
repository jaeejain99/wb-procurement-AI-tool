4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project closed on June 30, 2024, and the implementation progress (IP) and progress towards achieving the PDO

  were still assessed to be Satisfactory. This reflects the same ratings from the last ISR completed in January 2024 as no

  new activities were approved or initiated in the context of the available financing of the project. So far, four out of the six

  PDO indicators not only met but also exceeded their end targets. These successful indicators are (i) number of

  international arrivals identifying tourism as their main purpose of visit; (ii) annual growth rates in international tourists at

  targeted attraction sites; (iii) annual growth rate in domestic tourists at targeted attraction sites; and (iv) additional

  private investment generated in tourism-related activities within project target areas. The remaining two indicators have

  also made significant advancements and are marginally shy of reaching their end targets. These are (v) average length of

  stay in targeted destination areas by international tourists; and (vi) average length of stay in targeted destination areas by

  domestic tourists. As for the intermediate results indicators (IRIs), all five have achieved their end target. They include (i)

  Number of people trained in tourism services delivery/ of which 50% are women; (ii) Visitor satisfaction at sites with

  approved safety, access and/or quality improvements; (iii) Residents that are satisfied with tourism developments in

  targeted destination areas; (iv) Number of Private enterprises receiving matching grants /of which are 50% women

  owned or managed; and (v) Number of accredited tourism and hospitality training school. Relating to the IRI measuring

  the number of accredited tourism and hospitality training school, the contract was amended to strip down the

  deliverable to preparation feasibility study for the PPP School.

  The activities under the different components have substantially been completed with varying degrees of success and

  are rated following from the restructuring of the project in July 2022. The activities and their levels of achievement are

  detailed below:

      1. Component 1: Strengthening the tourism enabling environment (US$4.5 million) is rated Satisfactory (S):

           Originally allocated a budget of US$15 million, this component underwent significant scaling back during the July

           2022 restructuring. This was due to slow progress in activities including i) the skills sub-component, which covers

           low-level skills development and the Public-Private Partnership (PPP) tourism school; ii) the stalled progress on

           the aviation masterplan and the e-visa system, which were eventually dropped; and iii) a scaling back in the

           branding and marketing activities because of the impact of COVID-19 pandemic-induced travel restrictions which

           affected international tourists’ arrival. Notable achievements to date under this component include the rollout of

           television and media marketing campaigns by CNN and Expedia, a series of domestic marketing campaigns,

           capacity-building and training for key industry stakeholders, and training for trainers under the Lower-Level

           (technical) Tourism and Hospitality skills. The project has trained 1,830 people in tourism and hospitality against

           an end target of 1,000 people out of which 58% (1,061) being women. A consulting firm was recruited to provide

           transaction advisory towards the establishment of the proposed PPP Tourism School. Moreover, two tourism

           investment forums have been conducted, with a third expected to occur before the project's closing date.

      2. Component 2: Developing tourism sites and destination ($20 million) is rated Moderately Satisfactory

           (MS): The project restructuring in July 2022 increased the component allocation from US$15 million to US$20

           million. Between 2021 and 2023, the Ministry of Tourism, Arts, and Culture (MoTAC) prioritized seven larger

           iconic sites, overseeing their design, construction, and commissioning. Despite the government’s priority and the

           progress made under the project, several of the iconic sites—including the three national reserves (Shai Hills,

           Kakum, Mole), Cape Coast Castle and Elmina Castle, recognized as a United Nations Educational, Scientific, and

           Cultural Organization (UNESCO) world heritage site—only managed to complete critical studies and designs

           without advancing to the construction phase. It is noted that funds were not available in the project for follow-on

           construction. The designs and Environmental and Social (E&S) studies for these sites were completed in

          November 2023. Positively, the renovation of the flagship Kwame Nkrumah Mausoleum Park was successfully

          completed and inaugurated by the President of Ghana on July 4, 2023. Additionally, various tourist sites and

          destinations have been upgraded and these include the Bonwire Crafts Village, Bogyawe Palace Museum

          (Akwamufie), Ankasa Forest Reserve, Nzulezu Receptive Facility, and Gushegu Smock Village. Similarly,

          renovation works at smaller sites like Kintampo Falls and the Darmang Arts Training School have been completed.

          However, the proposed work on Fort St Antonio has been dropped due to delayed agreement with UNESCO on

          areas for renovation, time constraint and financing gap under this parent project.

       3. Component 3: Tourism enterprise support program ($10 million) is rated Satisfactory (S): The restructuring in

          June 2022 reallocated an additional US$5m towards the Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) grants program.

          Following this, the second round of grants managed by MOTAC was executed within the projected timeframe. As

          a result, the US$10m reallocated to the grants program in June 2022 was disbursed by early 2023, benefiting a

          total of 1,249 grantees across three windows: COVID-19 Recovery Grants (1,002 SMEs), SME Grants (206 SMEs),

          and Site Upgrade Grants (41 sites). Of these grant beneficiaries, 55 percent were females. The COVID-19

          Recovery Grants played a pivotal role for beneficiary SMEs, helping to alleviate some of the pandemic's adverse

          effects, especially for the female beneficiaries. Meanwhile, the SME Grants supported mid-sized firms’ new

          business ideas and plans, and the Site Upgrade Grants catered to tourism sites nationwide run by the private

          sector. To assure stakeholders of the efficient use of grant resources and the emerging impacts, a comprehensive

          end-of-grants evaluation and a fiduciary review for the entire grants program were successfully concluded. This

          was done to provide empirical evidence of the impact of the grants program and to assure stakeholders,

          including the Bank, of adherence to fiduciary standards.

       4. Component 4: Project Management and Institutional Strengthening (US$5.5 million) is rated Satisfactory (S): an

          additional US$500K was reallocated towards this Component after the July 2022 restructuring. The component

          supports MoTAC, particularly the project coordination unit (PCU), in managing and coordinating the project.

          Following the action plan that was agreed upon between the World Bank and MOTAC in April 2021 to revive the

          project, the PCU has performed creditably well. Additionally, the project's National Steering Committee (NSC)

          established to provide strategic advice, clear the annual workplan and budget and oversee the progress of

          implementation was reconstituted in June 2021, and has functioned efficiently.
