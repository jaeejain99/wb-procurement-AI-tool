 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The Ghana Productive Safety Nets (GPSNP-P164603) became effective on June 28, 2019, with the completion of hiring of all key staff and

  completion of initial drafts of the operational manuals. The project has also instituted a Project Technical Committee and a Project Oversight

  Committee, thereby meeting all Legal Requirements of the project. Project implementation is underway with the following updates:

  A Single Window Citizen Engagement Service (SWCES), which functions as a unified complaints and grievance mechanism for social protection

  programs and includes a 24-hour hotline, is operational and being extended nationwide.

  Preparation activities for initiating Labor-intensive Public Works (LIPW) activities on the ground have commenced, including: (i) selection and

  confirmation of the 80 districts to participate in the LIPW program; (ii) orientation of selected district assembly staff; (iii) completion of field appraisals

  sensitization activities for 31 implementing communities.

  The Livelihood Empowerment Against Poverty (LEAP) program, a targeted unconditional cash cash transfer program, continues to provide bi-

  monthly payments to beneficiary households, with 323,678 households paid in the 64th payment cycle in January 2020. The Ministry of Gender,

  Children and Social Protection has also successfully completed training to staff on e-monitoring and reporting to enhance service delivery.

  Preparation is underway to roll out productive inclusion activities to improve earnings and provide sustainable livelihoods to LEAP and LIPW

  program households. These activities will include a Complimentary Livelihood and Assets Support Scheme (CLASS) and linkages to agricultural

  support. CLASS has completed community selection in rural areas and, as of February 2020, commenced training of district staff who will supervise

  implementation.

  With respect to the Ghana National Household Registry (GNHR), preparations to conduct data collection in the Northern, North-Eastern and

  Savannah regions are underway. Memoranda of Understanding have been signed with the Ghana Statistical Service and the Information Services

  Department for their support with structural listing and spot checks, and public information campaign respectively. Data collection is expected to

  begin in April 2020.

  Note that due to the increasing COVID-19 infection risk in Ghana and the escalating countermeasures undertaken by the government, the design

  and implementation (including timetables) of activities under GPSNP may need to be adjusted. The government, the Bank, and other development

  partners are in discussions on options and considerations to continue to provide benefits and services to the poor in the coming months.
