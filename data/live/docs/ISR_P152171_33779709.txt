 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The Ghana Economic Management Strengthening (GEMS) project has progressed relatively well. The progress towards achievement of the PDO is

  Moderately Satisfactory to Satisfactory, with all four PDO level indicators achieving either Target Met or Partially Met. Six out of the twelve

  intermediate results indicators have been fully met, an improvement from three at the at the time of the last ISM in June 2021. Furthermore, the

  Project disbursed US$15.27 million, or 100.18% of the total commitment. Notable achievements include the establishment of the State Interests and

  Governance Authority (SIGA) and the passage of the Public Investment Management Regulations, 2020 (L.I. 2411), among others.

  Component 1: Strengthening Ghana Revenue Authority’s Business Intelligence Systems

  The objective of the component is to improve tax compliance, integrate income assessment regardless of source and location, simplify the

  processes, and align with international tax rules. The progress of the component is rated as Moderately Satisfactory. The full implementation of the

  Data Warehouse remains to be a challenge, and may not be achieved by project closing on March 31, 2022.

  Component 2: Strengthening Debt Management

  Component 2 aims to strengthen the Ministry of Finance’s (MoF) capacity to manage public debt with a high degree of transparency, as well as to

  improve treasury management and forecasting. The progress of the component is rated as Satisfactory. Progress has been made under

  Component 2, such as successfully strengthening capacity, developing and implementing Ghana’s Medium-Term Debt Management Strategy

  (MTDS).

  Component 3: Strengthening Capacity in Public Investment Management (PIM)

  The objective of Component 3 is to develop and institutionalize a functional, transparent process for public investment programming that is applied

  across the Government. The progress of the component is rated as Satisfactory. The component has seen positive achievements as highlighted by

  some of the indicators such as the increased percentage of public investment projects prioritized on the basis of published standard criteria for

  project selection (82% in 2021), the increased number of new projects reviewed by the PID team (46), and the number of civil servants receiving

  PIM related trainings (951, of which 287 are female).

  Component 4: Improving the Governance of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs)

  The objective of this component is to improve SOE governance by supporting reforms aimed at consolidating the state’s ownership role through an

  equity study and preparatory work for setting up a Single Entity responsible for overseeing SOEs; establishing the SOE Single Entity; and piloting

  corporate governance improvements in five SOEs. The progress of the component is rated as Satisfactory. The 2019 State Ownership Report

  (SOR) was published on the websites of SIGA and the MoF in July 2021, but the 2022 SOR is yet to be published. Dialogue is ongoing with the

  Ghana Audit Service for the implementation of measures to expediate the appointment of external auditors to enable SEs to comply with financial

  reporting requirements. In an effort to improve SEs transparency and disclosure a number of sensitization activities were conducted; resulting in an

  increase in the number of SEs that have published audited financial statements from 10 to 16.
