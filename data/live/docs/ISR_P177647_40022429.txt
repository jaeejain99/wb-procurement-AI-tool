3. KEY ISSUES & STATUS


 3.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions
   As the program entered its third year of implementation, key activities are progressing, including the Recovery and
   Accelerated Learning (ReAL) plan, integrated curriculum, mentoring program, pro-poor scholarships, and monitoring of
   minimum enabling conditions for early childhood development centers. Early evaluations and monitoring efforts have
   identified areas needing improvement to address implementation challenges. The government and Joint Financing
   Partners engaged an in-depth external review of the first 2.5 years of the 5-year School Education Sector Plan (SESP)
   implementation to identify successes and challenges and make necessary adjustments for the second half of the SESP
   implementation.

   Citizen Engagement.
   The Budget Review Meeting (BRM) 2025 for the SESP in early May invited representatives from Civil Society
   Organizations (CSOs), including the National Campaign for Education in Nepal (NCE-N) and the Association of INGOs in
   Nepal - Education Working Group (AIN-EWG). These representatives shared best practices, contributions, challenges, and

  constraints faced by stakeholders in accessing quality education, as well as potential ways forward. The discussions
  during the BRM provided an excellent opportunity for all education stakeholders to recognize the importance of ongoing
  dialogue between the government and CSOs. It also allowed for reflection on how CSOs can contribute to achieving the
  targets and commitments outlined in the SESP. It was agreed that MoEST will finalize and operationalize the SESP
  Community and Engagement Strategy by December 2025 to more effectively communicate and engage them in
  knowledge sharing and dissemination of good practices.

  The external audit report received an unqualified opinion but identified internal control issues, including discrepancies in
  reimbursable amounts and the absence of provisions for internal audits by Local Governments (LGs). An action plan was
  developed to address these issues, with some improvements noted. A team is established to assess the School
  Accounting System (SAS) and conduct the Annual Fiduciary Review (AFR), funded by the World Bank and Asian
  Development Bank (ADB), respectively. The SAS assessment team has begun field visits, with a draft report expected by
  the mid-June. Coordination with the Ministry of Federal Affairs and General Administration (MoFAGA) is emphasized to
  align with other projects like the Provincial and Local Governance Support Program (PLGSP) and Public Financial
  Management Reform and strengthen internal control, including internal audit in local governments and schools. MOEST
  agreed to identify funding to implement key actions to enhance financial management capacities of local governments
  and schools.
