 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  Overall Progress. The pace of project implementation has picked up following the lifting of Covid-related restrictions but is still well behind

  schedule overall. All the actions agreed during the previous mission in November-December 2021 have been completed albeit with some delays.

  The 2022 planting/rehabilitation design documents have been approved in all provinces; the eight project provinces have also initiated

  planting/rehabilitation activities with the total area covered over half of the revised target by end of August 2022. Disbursement remains low at US$

  11.65 million (8.09% of original credit amount; 14.6% of restructured amount) (as of June 30, 2022). This is largely because the majority of the

  awarded contracts (particularly infrastructure subprojects) are at the technical design stage, and the disbursement of these contracts is expected to

  increase when civil works get underway from August 2022.

  A total of 2,481 hectares of planting/rehabilitation has been completed as of August 31, 2022, which accounts for 53.11% of the revised target

  (4,671 hectares). As most provinces are currently in the middle of the 2022 planting season, this figure is expected to continue to increase

  throughout 2022. It is also noteworthy that Quang Tri was the first province to have completed all planned planting/rehabilitation in March 2021.

  Post-planting verification has been conducted for all planting/rehabilitation sites completed in 2020-21; the survival rate of the planted seedlings

  remains above the target threshold of 80 percent. The non-planting activities (small-scale infrastructure, technology and livelihood) have also

  progressed in the last six months but implementation varies substantially by province and overall has been slower than expected and will require

  close attention to ensure completion before project closing.

  Strong community engagement was observed in the project sites. Farmers were contracted to undertake planting and tending after receiving

  technical training. The interviewed farmers were happy with the added income and new skills they have acquired in the course of this work, as well

  as income from increased honey harvest thanks to the mangrove forests. Communities also benefit from improved forest protection infrastructure

  such as roads, which help to improve communities’ mobility and accessibility to markets, livelihood opportunities and other social services.
