 Implementation Status and Key Decisions



  The Kenya Cat DDO is a landmark operation which along with the Disaster Risk Finance Strategy is central to the Government’s National Disaster

  Risk Management (DRM) Framework. The operation was the first IDA Cat DDO and the first on mainland Africa. Following Board approval in June

  2018, the project was declared effective on November 27, 2018. During the implementation period Kenya experienced extreme events including

  two flood cycles, a landslide, locust invasion and COVID19. This occasioned the Government of Kenya (GoK) to make a partial disbursement of

  resources from the Cat DDO instrument in December 2019 (EUR. 63,114,235.00), with the balance disbursed in May 2020 (EUR. 102,285,765.00)

  to respond to extreme natural and health-related events.

  By the closing mission which concluded on September 30, 2021, of a total ten RIs, three had been achieved, these include (i) development of 6

  county hazards atlases, (ii) the establishment of the National Water Harvesting and Storage Authority and (iii) the production 5 consolidated

  post‐disaster expenditure reports. Further, one had been achieved but had slipped back to not achieved, while two are partially achieved, and four

  are significantly delayed.

                                      Agriculture Insurance Program (KAIP); ii) the Kenya Livestock Insurance Program (KLIP); iii) the Hunger

                                      Safety Net Program (HSNP), and iv) the Food Systems Project jointly implemented by the National

                                      Drought Management Authority (NDMA) and WFP.

                                      This results indicator slipped back to ‘not achieved’ during the May 2021 mission because of non-payment

                                      of premiums under the KAIP and the KLIP as budget reallocations were made to respond to Covid-19

                                      away from the State Department of Livestock (SDL) and Agriculture (SDA). The private sector understood

                                      the funding challenges, and allowed the GoK to pay these premiums in arrears. NT exerted significant

                                      effort to ensure both SDA and SDL received sufficient financing from the budget to pay premiums these

                                      outstanding premiums, bringing the KLIP and KAIP out of a crisis situation. That said, concerns were

                                      noted as to the sustainability of all four programs which deliver disaster relief in Kenya and the importance

                                      to National Treasury of ensuring sufficient budget is allocated to support these programs moving forward

                                      was impressed. Looking briefly at each program:

                                      Kenya Agriculture Insurance Program (KAIP): The the expected number of farmers insured this year

                                      under KAIP was c. 445,000 farmers however only 160,962 farmers actually purchased insurance due to

   Comments:

                                      delays in SDA receiving the required budgetary resources. This reduction means that the GoK will *not*

                                      achieve the overall target of 510,241 beneficiaries under RI 9.

                                      Kenya Livestock Insurance Program (KLIP): For the 2020/2021 insurance season sufficient budget was

                                      received, however the SDL was not allocated sufficient resources to finance the KLIP for the 2021/2022

                                      insurance season, placing a question mark over the financial sustainability of the program.

                                      Shock-responsive safety net – significant fiscal exposure to drought: Vegetative conditions in Northern

                                      Kenya during the 2020/2021 rainy seasons were relative good and so minimal payouts were triggered

                                      from the HSNP. However, it is understood that the HSNP still *does not* have a financing plan in place to

                                      fund drought response and therefore the cost of a scalability represents a significant contingent liability on

                                      the budget (up to Ksh 4 billion / US$40 million). The lack of financing for scalability transfers poses an

                                      imminent challenge with droughts forecast to impact northern Kenya. NT agreed to meet with the NDMA

                                      to review the financing plan for HSNP scalability as an immediate next step to address this issue.

                                      Although RI9 had been reported as achieved during the February 2021 Implementation Support Mission, it

                                      remains not achieved due to the reasons stated above.
